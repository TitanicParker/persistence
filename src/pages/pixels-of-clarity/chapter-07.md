# Chapter 7 — What Is Left When It Is Done?

*Pixels of Clarity*

[← Chapter 6](/persistence/pixels-of-clarity/chapter-06/) · **Chapter 7 of 18** · *The Third Question*

---

By now, the class had two questions they trusted.

The first was:

> **What kind of change happened?**

The second was:

> **What made that change enough?**

They could use the first question to find one of six families.

They could use the second to distinguish three different shapes of completion:

**Focal. Distributed. Release.**

That felt like a lot.

Then three sentence strips ruined everything.

Or, as the class had learned to say by now, improved everything.

---

## Three repairs

The three strips were:

> **The loose wheel is fixed in the correct position.**
>
> **The wheel is correct because it is aligned with the axle.**
>
> **The repair means the cart can roll properly again.**

Nobody had much trouble with the first question.

“Repair.”

All three.

Something had been failing relative to how it should be, and it had been put right enough.

The second question seemed easy too.

“What made the repair enough?”

They looked closely.

Each sentence was written so that the repair gathered around the wheel as the decisive repaired locus.

“Focal,” said someone.

So the board said:

| Sentence | Change | Completion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The loose wheel is fixed in the correct position. | Repair | Focal |
| The wheel is correct because it is aligned with the axle. | Repair | Focal |
| The repair means the cart can roll properly again. | Repair | Focal |

Someone folded their arms.

“Then they go in the same box.”

Another child shook her head.

“They don’t feel the same.”

“Why not?”

That was the question.

---

## Do not change the first two answers

The class had learned a useful trick.

When you wanted to discover a new difference, you tried not to let the old differences move around.

So they made a rule for the morning:

> **Do not change Repair. Do not change Focal. Find out what is still different.**

They read the first strip again.

> **The loose wheel is fixed in the correct position.**

“What do we have when that sentence is finished?”

“A fixed wheel.”

“Yes, but what about it?”

“The position is settled.”

“It is correct there.”

Someone wrote on a scrap of paper:

> **THE RESULT ITSELF**

Nobody knew whether that was a useful category yet.

They put it above the first sentence.

---

## The second sentence

> **The wheel is correct because it is aligned with the axle.**

“Same thing,” said someone. “The wheel is correct.”

“Maybe.”

“But if you take away the axle?”

“You can’t take away the axle. It’s a cart.”

“That’s not what I mean.”

The child tried again.

“What makes *correct* mean correct in this sentence?”

That changed the discussion.

The wheel was not merely left in a settled position.

Its correctness was given through how it stood **with** something else.

Aligned **with the axle**.

Someone drew two short lines on the board.

Then she made them parallel.

“The answer is still there because of how these stand together.”

A second scrap of paper appeared:

> **HOW IT STANDS WITH SOMETHING ELSE**

They put that above the second sentence.

---

## The third sentence

> **The repair means the cart can roll properly again.**

This one caused almost no argument.

“What is left when the repair is done?”

“The cart works.”

“More exactly?”

“It can roll.”

“That’s something it can do now.”

A third scrap appeared:

> **WHAT CAN HAPPEN NOW**

The three sentences were still all Repair.

They were still all Focal.

But now the board looked like this:

| Same change | Same completion shape | But what remains? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repair | Focal | the achieved result itself |
| Repair | Focal | a result that is what it is through a relation |
| Repair | Focal | a capacity or consequence now available |

The third difference had appeared.

---

## The third question

Nobody had a good short question for it yet.

“What is left?” was close, but it sounded as though they were asking what objects remained in the room.

“What happens after?” was not right either. A later event could be completely unrelated.

Eventually one child said:

“We want to know how the finished thing is still there.”

Another child changed one word.

“How the finished **result** is still there.”

The teacher wrote two versions on the board:

> **What is left when it is done?**

and underneath:

> **How does the achieved result remain available?**

The class preferred the first one.

The teacher preferred the second.

So, as before, they kept both.

---

## Is this really a third difference?

Three examples were not enough.

The class knew that now.

They needed to see whether the same distinction appeared somewhere else without changing the first two answers.

So they tried **Opening**.

The cards were:

> **The gate is now open.**
>
> **The gate is open because its latch is held clear of the catch.**
>
> **The open gate now lets the bicycles through.**

First question?

“Opening.”

Something previously unavailable or blocked had become available.

Second question?

They argued over the wording, then agreed that each example had been constructed so the completion gathered around the gate as the decisive locus.

“Focal.”

Third question?

They separated quickly.

> **The gate is now open.**

The achieved state itself remains.

> **The gate is open because its latch is held clear of the catch.**

The achieved state is what it is through a constitutive relation.

> **The open gate now lets the bicycles through.**

The achieved result remains as a changed capacity: passage can now occur.

Someone pointed at the three scraps from the wheel example.

“They work again.”

That mattered.

The class was not inventing three descriptions for three sentences.

They might be finding a third structural dimension.

They did not call it that yet.

---

## A trap: every sentence has relations

A child picked up this strip:

> **The gate is now open.**

“It still has relations,” he said. “The gate is in a doorway. It has hinges. It is open relative to the wall.”

All true.

But the class had already learned not to classify a sentence by everything that happened to be true around it.

They asked the third question again:

> **How does the achieved result remain available in this completion?**

The sentence gave them the achieved determination itself:

**the gate is open.**

The relation to a hinge might explain how a real gate works.

But it was not constitutive of the result as this sentence presented it.

Someone wrote a warning on the board:

> **A relationship being present is not enough.**

They underlined **enough**.

That word kept returning.

---

## Another trap: everything has effects

Then someone objected to the third pile.

“Everything has effects.”

A fixed wheel might make someone happy.

An aligned wheel might stop a crash.

An open gate might let in cold air.

If effects were everywhere, perhaps **WHAT CAN HAPPEN NOW** was useless.

The class tested the objection.

They compared:

> **The gate is now open.**

with:

> **The open gate now lets the bicycles through.**

The first sentence could have many consequences.

But it did not complete itself by giving one of those consequences as the achieved result.

The second did.

The ability to pass through was not merely something they could imagine afterward.

It was what the sentence gave them as the operative difference.

Another warning went on the board:

> **A possible consequence is not the same as a result that remains as capacity or effect.**

---

## The difficult middle pile

The middle pile caused the most trouble.

The children could easily hear:

**the result itself**

and:

**what it now does or allows.**

But the middle one was slippery.

So they collected more examples:

> **Her place in the line is second behind Maya.**
>
> **The price is fair relative to the amount received.**
>
> **The two pieces are correctly positioned because their edges meet evenly.**
>
> **His score counts as higher because it exceeds mine.**

“What is special about these?”

“The result needs something else.”

“Lots of results need something else.”

“It needs the other thing to be the result it is.”

That was better.

Second place is not second place without an ordering.

A proportion is not that proportion without the quantities that constitute it.

Alignment is alignment through how things stand together.

The result remains available **through a bearing** among things.

Nobody used the word *bearing* yet.

But the shape was becoming clear.

---

## Three unnamed ways of remaining

At lunchtime the board had three columns.

The children had renamed them several times.

The latest version said:

### A — IT STANDS

The achieved determination itself is what remains available.

### B — IT HOLDS THROUGH HOW THINGS STAND TOGETHER

The achieved result remains what it is through a constitutive relation, dependency, contrast, condition, source, proportion, frame, support, or mutual determination.

### C — IT WORKS ONWARD

The achieved result remains as a capacity, operative effect, changed continuation, rule, function, or difference in what can happen.

The second heading was much too long.

Everyone agreed about that.

But shortening it too early would only hide what they had discovered.

So they left it alone.

---

## Try it without the names

Sort these nine sentences by the **third question only**.

Do not worry yet about giving the three groups official names.

1. **The answer is now fixed at twelve.**
2. **Her position is third relative to the other runners.**
3. **The new bridge now carries traffic across the river.**
4. **The boundary is now clearly established.**
5. **The two payments are equal in value.**
6. **The repaired pump can move water again.**
7. **The agreement now stands as the decision.**
8. **The meaning depends on how the word is used in this sentence.**
9. **The new rule lets every player take a turn.**

For each one, ask:

> **How does the achieved result remain available?**

Is it mainly:

- the achieved determination itself;
- something that remains what it is through a constitutive bearing;
- something that remains as operative capacity or consequence?

Do not use keywords.

Explain the result.

---

## The three questions together

At the end of the day, someone rewrote the board.

Not the teacher.

A child.

She wrote:

### Question 1

> **What kind of change happened?**

### Question 2

> **What made that change enough?**

### Question 3

> **How does the achieved result remain available?**

Then she stood back.

“Is that it?” someone asked.

“What do you mean?”

“Are those the three questions?”

Nobody knew yet.

But they had something they had not had at the beginning of the book.

They could take two sentences that looked almost identical and ask three different structural questions of them.

And each question could reveal a difference the others could not.

The teacher looked at the three long temporary headings for the third question.

“You know what happens next.”

The class did.

They had already done it twice.

First you see the distinction.

Then, when you can recognize it without the label, you earn the shorter name.
