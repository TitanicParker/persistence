# Chapter 9 — Three Ways the Result Remains

*Pixels of Clarity*

[← Chapter 8](/persistence/pixels-of-clarity/chapter-08/) · **Chapter 9 of 18** · *The Third Dimension Gets Its Names*

---

The four question-piles from yesterday were still on the wall.

**WE CAN ANSWER THIS NOW**

**WE COULD TEST THIS**

**KEEP THIS QUESTION**

**NOT YET**

Under them were the questions the class had made for itself.

> **Did we invent the patterns or notice them?**
>
> **Are the patterns in the words, in what happened, or in how we understand what happened?**
>
> **Does everything have one of these patterns?**
>
> **Could something fit perfectly and still be wrong?**
>
> **Why are there three questions?**

Nobody had solved those overnight.

That was fine.

Mr Vale did not take them down.

Instead, he wrote three of the children's old temporary headings on the board:

> **IT STANDS**
>
> **IT HOLDS THROUGH HOW THINGS STAND TOGETHER**
>
> **IT WORKS ONWARD**

Maya laughed.

"The middle one still needs help."

"It does," said Priya.

"But we know what it means now."

That was the important part.

The class had already discovered the difference.

Today they were only going to earn the shorter names.

---

## The first name

Mr Vale placed three cards on the table.

> **The answer is fixed at twelve.**
>
> **The boundary is now established here.**
>
> **The agreement now stands as the decision.**

"Third question only," said Jonah.

Nobody had to remind him.

They already knew the first two questions were not the issue.

The question now was:

> **How does the achieved result remain available?**

Priya tapped the first sentence.

"The answer is twelve. That's just what has been settled."

Maya pointed to the boundary sentence.

"Same kind of thing. The completed determination itself is what we've got."

Leo looked at the temporary heading.

"So: it stands."

Mr Vale turned over the first formal card.

> **Standing**

Nobody looked surprised.

"That's almost suspiciously sensible," said Leo.

The class wrote:

> **Standing: the achieved determination itself remains available as the result.**

Then they shortened it for themselves:

> **Standing: what was settled is what stands.**

Mr Vale left both versions on the board.

---

## Standing is not just staying

Sara immediately found the trap.

"So Standing means it stays there."

Mr Vale picked up a chair and put it in the corner.

The chair stayed there.

"Standing?"

"No," said the class.

"Why not?"

"Because we aren't talking about whether an object physically stays in the room."

Maya added:

"We're asking what kind of result the completion leaves available."

Exactly.

A thing can remain physically present without being **Standing** in this technical sense.

Standing is about the achieved determination itself being the persisting result.

The class added a warning:

> **Standing does not mean merely ‘it stayed’.**

---

## The second name

The middle pile was harder.

Mr Vale put down:

> **Her place is third relative to the other runners.**
>
> **The two payments are equal in value.**
>
> **The wheel is correct because it is aligned with the axle.**

"What remains?" asked Sara.

"A relation," said Leo.

"Careful," said Maya. "We said yesterday that just having a relation somewhere isn't enough."

They tried again.

What made third place third?

Not a number floating by itself.

It was third **in an ordering**.

What made the two payments equal?

Not either payment by itself.

The equality existed through how the two amounts stood to one another.

What made the wheel correct in that sentence?

Its alignment with the axle.

Jonah looked at the temporary heading.

"The result is what it is because of how things bear on one another."

Mr Vale turned over the second formal card.

> **Bearing**

Priya frowned.

"Bearing like carrying something?"

"Not here."

"Bearing like ‘what does this have to do with that’?"

"Closer."

They wrote:

> **Bearing: the achieved result remains what it is through a constitutive relation, dependency, contrast, condition, source, frame, support, proportion, or mutual determination.**

Leo stared at the sentence.

"We are absolutely keeping a shorter version."

They did:

> **Bearing: the result holds through how things stand with one another.**

That was enough for now.

---

## Bearing is not merely ‘there is a relationship’

Mr Vale wrote:

> **The red cup is beside the blue cup, and the red cup is full.**

"There is a relationship," said Sara. "Beside."

"Does that make the fullness Bearing?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because the cup isn't full *through* being beside the other cup."

Exactly.

A relation can be present without constituting the achieved result.

Then Mr Vale wrote:

> **This runner is second because only Maya is ahead of her.**

Now the ordering is not background scenery.

It is part of what makes the result the result it is.

The class added another warning:

> **Bearing does not mean merely ‘there is a relationship’.**

---

## The third name

The last pile was the easiest to hear but the easiest to overuse.

> **The repaired pump can move water again.**
>
> **The new bridge carries traffic across the river.**
>
> **The new rule lets every player take a turn.**

"What remains?" asked Mr Vale.

"Something works," said Priya.

"Something can happen now," said Maya.

"The result has an effect," said Jonah.

Mr Vale turned over the final card.

> **Efficacy**

"That sounds like medicine," said Leo.

"It means effective capacity or operation," said Mr Vale.

"Can we say ‘it does something now’?"

"As a first handle, yes."

The class wrote:

> **Efficacy: the achieved result remains as an accomplished capacity, operative effect, changed continuation, rule, function, or consequential difference in what can happen.**

Then the shorter version:

> **Efficacy: the result remains as something that now works onward.**

---

## Efficacy is not merely usefulness

Maya found the trap this time.

"A correct answer is useful. Does that make it Efficacy?"

"No," said Jonah.

"Why not?"

"Because useful isn't the question."

They compared:

> **The answer is twelve.**

and

> **That answer now lets us calculate the next stage.**

The first gives the achieved determination itself.

The second gives an operative consequence as part of the completed result.

Standing versus Efficacy.

Same number.

Different persistence.

The class wrote:

> **Efficacy does not mean merely ‘something useful happened’.**

---

## Three names for a difference we already knew

The board now looked much cleaner.

| What the class first noticed | Formal name |
| --- | --- |
| The achieved result itself stands | **Standing** |
| The result holds through how things stand with one another | **Bearing** |
| The result remains as capacity, effect, rule, or changed continuation | **Efficacy** |

Sara read the three words aloud.

"Standing. Bearing. Efficacy."

Then she looked back at the three temporary headings.

"The big headings are easier."

"At first," said Maya.

"The short names are easier once you know what they are compressing."

Mr Vale did not say anything.

The children no longer needed him to explain why naming came after discovery.

---

## Test them where the topic changes

The class had learned not to trust a distinction that worked only on one kind of example.

So they changed subject matter quickly.

### A decision

> **The decision is final.**

The achieved determination itself remains.

**Standing.**

> **The decision holds because both sides accepted the same terms.**

The result is constituted through a bearing among parties and terms.

**Bearing.**

> **The decision now authorizes the next step.**

The result remains as operative consequence.

**Efficacy.**

### A distinction

> **The two signals are now clearly different.**

**Standing.**

> **The distinction is fixed by how each signal compares with the reference.**

**Bearing.**

> **The distinction now lets the machine route them correctly.**

**Efficacy.**

### An opening

> **The route is now open.**

**Standing.**

> **The route counts as open because its connected sections now form an unbroken way through.**

**Bearing.**

> **The opening now allows traffic to pass.**

**Efficacy.**

The topic kept changing.

The third distinction kept surviving.

Priya looked at the question wall from Chapter 8.

"That's evidence for something."

"For what?" asked Leo.

"I don't know yet."

She moved nothing from **KEEP THIS QUESTION**.

That was becoming a skill too.

---

## The third structural question finally has its answers

The class rewrote the board.

### Question 1

> **What kind of change happened?**

Six answers:

- Differentiation
- Opening
- Relating
- Repair
- Integration
- Settlement

### Question 2

> **What made that change count as complete?**

Three answers:

- Focal
- Distributed
- Release

### Question 3

> **How does the achieved result remain available?**

Three answers:

- Standing
- Bearing
- Efficacy

Jonah counted the formal words.

"Six plus three plus three. Twelve."

Maya shook her head.

"Fifteen."

"Right."

Leo was looking at the numbers differently.

"No. Wait."

He wrote:

> **6**
>
> **3**
>
> **3**

Then he drew multiplication signs between them.

> **6 × 3 × 3**

Sara saw it immediately.

"Don't say it."

"Why not?"

"Because every time we count too early, something gets weird."

Leo looked at Mr Vale.

Mr Vale shrugged.

"You may multiply numbers whenever you like."

Leo did.

Then stopped.

"Fifty-four."

The class went quiet.

Mr Vale still did not draw any boxes.

---

## Does that mean fifty-four kinds of thing?

That was the first question.

"Fifty-four kinds of sentence?" asked Sara.

"Fifty-four kinds of event?" asked Jonah.

"Fifty-four kinds of understanding?" asked Maya.

"Fifty-four boxes?" said Leo.

Mr Vale pointed to the question wall.

"Where would you put those?"

Nobody chose **WE CAN ANSWER THIS NOW**.

Priya put:

> **WHAT DOES 6 × 3 × 3 ACTUALLY COUNT?**

under **NOT YET**.

Then she moved it.

"No. We can test some of it."

She placed it under:

> **WE COULD TEST THIS**

That seemed right.

The number had appeared.

But the map had not.

---

## A dangerous picture

Leo drew three dials.

The first had six positions.

The second had three.

The third had three.

"There," he said. "That's the machine."

Maya looked at it.

"What machine?"

"You set the first dial, then the second, then the third, and that builds the result."

Mr Vale looked up immediately.

"Does it?"

Leo hesitated.

The class looked at the three questions.

They had always asked them one after another because that was a useful way to search.

But was the thing itself made in three stages?

Sara shook her head slowly.

"I don't think the wheel gets repaired, then becomes Focal, then gets Standing stuck onto it."

"Exactly," said Maya. "That's ridiculous when you say it like that."

Leo erased the arrows between his dials.

"So the questions are in an order for us."

"Maybe," said Priya.

"But the thing we're finding isn't built in that order."

Mr Vale wrote one sentence on the board:

> **The questions help us find the box. They are not instructions for building what happened.**

Nobody knew exactly what the box was yet.

But they knew what mistake the next chapter would have to prevent.

---

## Your turn: name only the third answer

For each sentence, ignore the first two coordinates for now.

Ask only:

> **How does the achieved result remain available?**

1. **The final score is 3–2.**
2. **Her rank is fourth relative to the rest of the field.**
3. **The repaired switch now turns the lights on.**
4. **The boundary now stands at the river.**
5. **The price is fair because it matches the amount supplied.**
6. **The new opening lets water flow through.**
7. **The agreement is now final.**
8. **The two parts are correctly placed because their edges align.**
9. **The new rule now allows the game to continue.**

Choose:

- Standing
- Bearing
- Efficacy

Then explain why the nearest alternative is wrong.

That last step matters more than the label.

---

## Keep this question

Before leaving, Maya added one more card to the wall:

> **IF THE THREE QUESTIONS ARE DIFFERENT, HOW CAN THEY ALL BE ABOUT ONE THING?**

Nobody tried to answer it.

Mr Vale put it under:

> **WE COULD TEST THIS**

That was tomorrow's work.

## Next: Three Questions, One Thing

We now have all fifteen formal names.

We also have a number waiting in the corner of the board:

**54.**

But before we turn that number into a map, we have to stop one serious mistake.

The three questions are a route into the structure.

They are not three pieces from which the structure is assembled.
