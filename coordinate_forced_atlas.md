# The Constraint Grammar of Completed Intelligibility

## Architecture

This atlas represents **54 recurrent forms of situational completion** generated by the intersection of three structurally distinct constraints:

**Transformation Pattern × Completion Topology × Persistence Mode = 6 × 3 × 3 = 54 coordinate positions.**

The atlas does not classify subject matter. It models recurrent ways in which an initially unresolved situation can become sufficiently determinate for intelligible uptake. Each cell therefore has two levels:

- **Coordinate-generated form** — the most abstract local form demanded by the three coordinates themselves.
- **Canonical crystallization** — a sharper semantic realization that makes that generated form phenomenologically recognizable.

Where the coordinates leave almost no latitude, the two levels nearly coincide. Where they leave a wider admissible region, the distinction is retained explicitly rather than forcing a specific crystallization to masquerade as coordinate necessity.

### Constraint-strength scale

- **Near-determinate** — the coordinate triple leaves little beyond wording, domain, or minor semantic specification.
- **Tightly constrained** — the triple defines a narrow local family; the crystallization adds a significant but strongly guided semantic determination.
- **Open realization** — the triple fixes a recognizable phenomenological syntax, but the crystallization is one realization within a broader admissible region.

## Primitive dimensions

### 1. Transformation Pattern

The **Transformation Pattern** specifies what kind of unresolvedness is altered between the opening and completed situation.

| Value | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Differentiation** | A difference that was unavailable, flattened, conflated, or non-operative becomes determinate enough to matter. |
| **Opening** | Availability, reach, continuation, or possibility becomes greater than it was under the opening constraint. |
| **Relating** | A bearing, dependency, correspondence, ordering, or mutual determination becomes constitutive of the situation. |
| **Repair** | An organization that fails relative to an operative criterion is redirected, reweighted, replaced, or released from the fault. |
| **Integration** | Parts acquire determinate roles through organization into a whole and thereby gain structure, mutual support, or joint capacity. |
| **Settlement** | A live demand for further uptake, choice, explanation, completion, or adjudication is discharged. |

### 2. Completion Topology

The **Completion Topology** specifies the structural shape by which the transformation counts as complete.

| Value | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Focal** | Completion gathers around a privileged locus: a selected item, source, target, connection, whole, or settlement point. |
| **Distributed** | Completion requires a constitutive plurality, span, sequence, range, reciprocal field, or system; no single locus suffices by itself. |
| **Release** | Completion occurs because some prior governance loses its hold: conflation, restriction, mismatch, fault, imbalance, or unresolved demand is no longer determinative. |

### 3. Persistence Mode

The **Persistence Mode** specifies how the achieved completion remains available for subsequent uptake.

| Value | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Standing** | The achieved determination itself remains available as something that now stands. |
| **Bearing** | The achieved result persists only through a constitutive relation, dependency, contrast, condition, frame, source, proportion, or mutual support. |
| **Efficacy** | The achieved result persists as an accomplished capacity, operative effect, changed continuation, or functional consequence. |

The resulting syntax is therefore:

> **transformation-pattern + completion-pattern + persistence-pattern**

Every row below should be readable as one conjugated instance of that grammar.
