# Chapter 8 — Why Does This Keep Showing Up?

The next morning, there were no sentence strips on the tables.

That was the first thing everyone noticed.

For weeks, the strips had been everywhere. In piles. In envelopes. Under books. Once, somehow, inside Maya's lunchbox.

Today the tables were empty.

On the board were the three questions the class had written the day before:

> **What kind of change happened?**
>
> **What made that change enough?**
>
> **How does the achieved result remain available?**

Nobody had erased them.

Mr Vale was at his desk, fixing the loose arm on a pair of glasses.

He did not say anything about the board.

He tightened a tiny screw, folded the glasses, opened them again, and put them on.

"Fixed," said Maya.

Mr Vale looked up.

"What?"

"Your glasses. You fixed them."

"Did I?"

"Obviously."

"How do you know?"

Maya stared at him.

"Because they were broken and now they're not."

Jonah pointed at the board.

"Repair."

A few people laughed.

"You can't help it now," said Priya.

"Help what?"

"Doing the questions."

Jonah shrugged.

"It was a repair. The screw was the place that had to be put right. And now the arm stays where it's supposed to."

Maya looked at him.

"You just did all three."

"No I didn't."

"You basically did."

Jonah looked back at the board.

He had.

Mr Vale took the glasses off again.

"Interesting," he said.

Then he put them in his pocket.

---

At break, Priya and Leo were arguing about the football game.

Not a serious argument. The sort where both people are still eating crisps.

"It was out," said Priya.

"It wasn't."

"It crossed the line."

"Half of it crossed the line."

"That's still out."

"No, the whole ball has to cross."

Maya, who was sitting on the wall beside them, said, "You're trying to settle it."

Priya stopped chewing.

"What?"

"You're both trying to get to a point where the question doesn't need deciding anymore."

Leo looked suspicious.

"Are you doing school at us?"

"No."

"That sounded like school."

"I just noticed it."

There was a pause.

Then Priya said, "She's right, though."

Leo groaned.

---

After break, Mr Vale put five things on the front table.

A toy car with one wheel beside it.

A knotted shoelace.

A sheet containing the rules for a playground game.

A bowl, a spoon, and the ingredients for a simple dressing.

And a hand-drawn map with a bridge marked CLOSED.

There were still no sentence strips.

"What are we doing?" asked Jonah.

"I don't know," said Mr Vale.

That got everyone's attention.

"You brought the stuff," said Maya.

"I did."

"So you must know."

"I know what I brought. I don't know what you're going to notice."

He pointed to the board.

"Yesterday you asked whether those were the three questions."

"Are they?" said Leo.

Mr Vale picked up the toy car.

"I don't know how much that question means yet."

"You're the teacher," said Leo.

"Yes."

"Aren't you supposed to know?"

"Sometimes."

He put the car down.

"Today I'd rather find out what the question is asking."

---

The class split into groups.

Their instruction was almost annoyingly short:

> **Do something with one of the things. Then see whether our questions still work.**

"That's not an instruction," said Priya.

"It has a verb," said Mr Vale.

"Barely."

Priya's group took the toy car.

The wheel had come off because a small plastic pin had slipped out. They pushed the wheel back into place, found the pin under the tray, and pressed it through the axle.

Then they rolled the car across the table.

"Repair," said Jonah.

"Too easy," said Priya.

"The wheel was the place where it had to be fixed," said Maya.

"And what remains?" asked Jonah.

Priya rolled the car back toward him.

"It can do this again."

Nobody wrote anything down for a moment.

"That's weird," Maya said.

"What is?"

"There isn't a sentence."

They all looked at the car.

The car rolled off the edge of the table.

Jonah caught it.

"Still repair," he said.

---

At the next table, Leo's group had chosen the map.

The bridge was closed, but there was another path drawn around the river.

Mr Vale had given them a pencil and one extra piece of information: a footbridge had opened farther north.

They added it.

"Opening," Leo said immediately.

"Why?" asked Sara.

"Because a route that wasn't available is available."

"But that's just the map."

Leo frowned.

"No. The map is telling us the route is available."

"So is the opening on the paper or at the river?"

"At the river."

"But there isn't a river."

"There is in the thing the map is about."

Sara tapped the pencil against the table.

"So we're not sorting the pencil marks."

Leo looked toward the board.

"We never were."

---

The dressing caused more trouble.

Oil, lemon juice, mustard, salt.

At first the ingredients sat separately in the bowl.

Then Maya whisked them.

"Integration," said Priya.

"Maybe," said Maya.

"Obviously integration."

"But is it integration because we used the word *dressing*?"

"We didn't use the word dressing."

"Mr Vale did."

"He called it ingredients for a dressing."

"Exactly. What if he'd just put the things there and said nothing?"

They looked at the bowl.

Priya dipped the spoon in and tasted it.

"Still dressing."

"That isn't what I mean."

"I know. But it's good."

Maya took the spoon away from her.

"I'm asking whether the pattern comes from the words or from what happened."

Jonah, from the next table, said, "Maybe both."

"How can it be both?"

"The words let you say what happened."

"That's not the same as causing it."

"I didn't say causing."

Mr Vale was walking past.

He stopped.

"Do you want me to answer that?"

The group went quiet.

Maya thought about it.

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because I don't think we know the question properly yet."

Mr Vale nodded once and kept walking.

---

By lunchtime, the board had become untidy.

Nobody had been asked to write on it, but people had.

Under the three questions were examples:

**glasses**

**football argument**

**toy car**

**new footbridge**

**dressing**

**shoelace**

**game rule**

Then Sara had drawn a line underneath them and written:

> **NONE OF THESE WERE SENTENCE STRIPS**

Leo added:

> **BUT THE QUESTIONS STILL WORKED**

And underneath that, in smaller writing:

> **WHY?**

That was the part everyone kept looking at.

---

After lunch, Mr Vale did something he had not done before.

He gave every group a large sheet of paper with four headings.

**WE CAN ANSWER THIS NOW**

**WE COULD TEST THIS**

**KEEP THIS QUESTION**

**NOT YET**

"What's *not yet*?" asked Leo.

"A question that might be good but that we don't yet have enough to answer."

"How do we know we don't have enough?"

"That can be one of the things you argue about."

Maya smiled.

"So now we're sorting questions."

"Apparently."

"Into piles."

Mr Vale looked at her.

"Apparently."

She laughed.

"We're back at Chapter One."

"Chapter what?"

"Never mind."

---

The first question came from Jonah.

> **Do the patterns only work on sentences?**

Everyone agreed quickly.

"We can answer that," said Priya. "No."

"Can we?" said Sara.

"We just did the car and the map and the dressing."

"But we talked about them using sentences."

That slowed everyone down.

"You can't think about something without saying anything about it," said Leo.

"You can," said Maya. "You can catch a ball."

"That's not thinking about it."

"How do you know?"

"Because—"

Leo stopped.

"Oh, this is going to be annoying."

They moved the question from **WE CAN ANSWER THIS NOW** to **WE COULD TEST THIS**.

---

Priya wrote the next one.

> **Did we invent the patterns or notice them?**

Nobody spoke for several seconds.

"Keep it," said Maya.

"Definitely keep it," said Jonah.

They put it under **KEEP THIS QUESTION**.

---

Sara wrote:

> **Are the patterns in the words or in what happened?**

"Both," said Leo.

"You said that before."

"Jonah said it before. I'm saying it now."

"That doesn't make it right."

"I know."

They argued for five minutes and got nowhere.

Eventually Sara drew an arrow from the question to **KEEP THIS QUESTION**.

Then she added:

> **OR IN HOW WE UNDERSTAND WHAT HAPPENED?**

Nobody moved that one anywhere.

They simply stared at it.

"Not yet," Maya said eventually.

"Why?"

"Because I don't even know what an answer would look like."

That seemed like a good reason.

They put it under **NOT YET**.

---

The questions multiplied.

> **Have we always been doing this without knowing?**

> **Could two people put the same thing in different places?**

> **Does everything have one of these patterns?**

> **Could something fit perfectly and still be wrong?**

> **Why are there three questions?**

> **Are there more than three?**

> **Why do the same kinds of answer keep coming back?**

That last one stopped them.

It was close to Leo's **WHY?** on the board, but better.

Maya read it aloud.

"Why do the same kinds of answer keep coming back?"

Jonah pointed at the six names they already knew.

"We found six answers to the first question."

Priya pointed at the three names from the second.

"And three answers to that one."

Then everyone looked at the third question.

They still did not have names for its answers.

But they had seen three shapes there too.

"Three again," said Sara.

"Maybe," said Maya.

"We saw three."

"That's not the same as knowing there are only three."

Mr Vale, at the back of the room, smiled but said nothing.

---

Near the end of the day, the class returned to the toy car.

Mr Vale placed it in the middle of the floor.

"Suppose we had never spoken about this car," he said.

"Fine," said Leo.

"Suppose the wheel came off, Priya repaired it, and the car rolled again."

Priya rolled it.

"Did something happen?"

"Yes."

"Did the words make it happen?"

"No," said several people.

"Then have we proved that Repair exists in the world without us?"

The room went quiet.

It sounded as though the answer should be yes.

But nobody said yes.

Maya picked up the car.

"We've proved the wheel came off and got put back."

"Good."

"And that *Repair* is a good way of understanding that."

"Good."

"But I don't know whether that means Repair is sitting inside the car."

Leo laughed.

"Tiny Repair hiding next to the axle."

"You know what I mean."

"I do."

Mr Vale pointed at the four headings.

"Where does the bigger question go?"

Maya walked to the board.

Under **KEEP THIS QUESTION**, she wrote:

> **ARE THESE PATTERNS IN THE WORLD, OR IN THE WAY WE UNDERSTAND THE WORLD?**

She stepped back.

Then Priya came up and added underneath:

> **OR IS THAT THE WRONG CHOICE?**

Mr Vale read it.

"Keep both," he said.

---

Nobody had learned a new name that day.

There was no new list to memorize.

No new pile of cards.

No new answer written in a box.

Yet when the bell went, nobody packed up immediately.

They were still looking at the board.

The strange thing was not that their three questions worked on sentence strips.

The strange thing was that they kept working when the strips disappeared.

A broken pair of glasses.

A football argument.

A toy car.

A route across a river.

A bowl of dressing.

Different things.

Different words.

Different problems.

And still the same questions kept finding something to ask.

Jonah was halfway through the door when he turned around.

"Sir?"

"Yes?"

"Yesterday we asked if those were the three questions."

"You did."

"I think we asked it wrong."

Mr Vale waited.

Jonah looked at the board.

"We should have asked why there are three kinds of answer to the last one too."

Then he left.

Mr Vale looked at the third question:

> **How does the achieved result remain available?**

Under it were three unnamed marks the children had carried forward from yesterday.

He did not name them.

Not yet.

---

## Keep This Question

> **Are these patterns in the world, or in the way we understand the world?**

We have enough evidence to ask the question.

We do not yet have enough to close it.

## Test It Somewhere Else

Before the next chapter, notice one thing outside school that becomes complete in some way: a decision, a repair, an agreement, a game, a plan, a route, a task, a disagreement, a recipe, or something else.

Do not start by trying to name its box.

Start with the three questions.

See whether they have anything to ask.

## Next

The class has already seen three different answers to its third question.

Now it is time to find out whether those differences are stable enough to deserve names.
