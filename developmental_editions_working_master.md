## Purpose

Build thirteen approximately 3,000-word essays, one for each developmental year from **6 through 18**, as recursive higher-resolution articulations of the entire Constraint Grammar of Completed Intelligibility.

The existing Five-Year-Old Edition is the baseline.

Each later essay:
- re-articulates the whole theory;

- introduces no simplification that later has to be unlearned;

Every earlier explanation should remain true enough that the mature version can say:

## 3. Adult audience, developmental language

The reader is always an adult.

The hidden age controls:
- vocabulary;
- sentence length;
- abstraction;
- inferential distance;
- number of distinctions held simultaneously;
- degree of qualification;
- technical terminology;
- epistemic modality.

It does **not** license:
- baby talk;
- childish jokes;
- fake child dialogue;
- condescension;
- schoolroom tone.

## 4. Stable furniture

The essays should feel like repeated passes through the same intellectual room.

Prefer recurring corpus examples over novelty.

Examples should gain resolution as the series proceeds.

## 5. Repository-governed theory

Use the corpus as governing source:
- canonical monograph;

- Research Status;

- intellectual history;

Do not silently substitute outside theory.

# Draft: The Six-Year-Old Edition

## How Does Something Become Finished Enough to Let Us Go On?

Suppose the red ball is still under one of three cups.

You know the little puzzle already. There are three cups on the table. Somewhere under one of them is the ball. Until a cup is lifted, something is still missing. You can look at the cups. You can guess. You can point. But the question has not yet been answered.

Then the middle cup is lifted.

There is the ball.

At first this seems almost too simple to study. A question was open, and now it is answered. But there is a useful detail hiding inside that small change. The important thing is not only that something happened. The important thing is that the thing that still needed to happen has happened **enough**.

That word matters.

A game can stop without being finished. A bicycle can stop moving because its chain has fallen off. A conversation can stop because somebody leaves the room. A journey can stop because the road is blocked. In all of those cases, activity has ended for the moment, but the problem that was still open may remain open.

So when we say that something has become complete, we do not simply mean that it stopped.

We mean that the particular unfinished part has received enough of the right kind of answer that it no longer asks for the same thing.

That is the small difference the earlier account has left us ready to see more clearly.

### An unfinished situation already tells us something

Imagine a locked door.

You want to go through it. The door is closed and locked. That is not the same problem as a broken bicycle, a pile of loose parts, or a choice between two roads.

The locked door is asking for access.

A broken wheel is asking for repair.

Two things that look the same may be asking for a difference to be made clear.

Two pieces that do not yet belong together may be asking for a useful relation.

A box full of bicycle parts may be asking for organization into a whole.

A choice between two live possibilities may be asking for a decision.

The unfinished situation is therefore not empty. It already has a shape.

The theory calls that shape **opening pressure**. We can keep the name because the idea behind it is simple: something is still required, and the kind of thing required helps tell us what kind of completion would count.

If the door is locked, painting the door blue does not answer the problem. If the wheel is broken, choosing chocolate instead of vanilla does not repair it. If you need to know which key opens the lock, piling all the keys into a neat row may help you look at them, but the row itself is not yet the answer.

The problem places a demand on what can finish it.

### Six different kinds of change

The theory says that six broad kinds of change appear again and again when an unfinished situation becomes complete enough.

The first is **Differentiation**.

Imagine a crowded keyring. Many keys are there, but you need one particular key. Someone points and says, “This one.” A difference that mattered only vaguely, or did not matter yet, becomes clear enough to guide what happens next.

Or look at a photograph with many faces. Someone points to one face. That person now stands as the person being talked about.

Differentiation is the change in which a difference becomes important enough to count.

The second is **Opening**.

A road is blocked, but a side road goes around the blockage and joins the route again. The important change is not that one thing has been selected. The important change is that going farther has become possible.

A wall has no gate. Then a gate is made. A dark room has very little visible in it. Then a lamp is switched on, and a field becomes available from that source.

Opening is the change in which something that could not continue, reach, or become available now can.

The third is **Relating**.

A rope lies loose between two boats. Then it is tied from one boat to the other. A relation now stands between them.

But a relation does not have to be a rope. One person calls and another answers. Two dancers begin adjusting to one another. Recipe steps are put into an order. A sensor is connected to an alarm.

Relating is the change in which one thing begins to bear on another in a way that matters to the situation.

The fourth is **Repair**.

A toy robot does not work because its battery is the wrong way around. The battery is turned. The bad arrangement no longer controls what the robot can do.

A bicycle brake rubs against the wheel. The caliper is adjusted. The fault that was governing the bicycle’s movement stops governing it.

Repair is not just “making something nice.” There has to be some way in which the arrangement was failing for the task at hand. Repair changes that failing arrangement so the fault is no longer in charge.

The fifth is **Integration**.

A box contains a frame, two wheels, a chain, pedals, bearings, and many smaller pieces. Having all the parts is not yet the same as having a bicycle.

When the parts are put together, their roles change. A wheel is no longer merely a wheel beside other things. It becomes a wheel **of the bicycle**. The chain becomes part of a larger working arrangement.

Integration is the change in which separate parts acquire roles through a whole.

The sixth is **Settlement**.

Several choices are still live. You keep considering them. Nothing is wrong with any one object, and nothing necessarily needs to be joined or repaired. The unfinished part is the need to decide.

Then you choose.

The important change is that you no longer owe the same act of choosing.

Settlement is the change in which a live demand for another step is discharged.

These six changes are called **Transformation Patterns**.

They answer one question:

**What kind of unfinishedness changed?**

### The same change can become enough in different ways

Now suppose we know what kind of change occurred.

We are not finished yet.

Think about a classroom becoming quiet.

Perhaps almost everyone is quiet already, and one child is still talking. That child stops. The change gathers around one important place.

Or perhaps the whole room is noisy, and children across the room gradually settle. No single child is enough. The quiet has to spread across the group.

Or perhaps the room is noisy because a loud game is still running. The game ends. The thing that had been keeping the noise going is no longer in charge.

These are three different ways in which a change can become enough.

The theory calls them **Focal**, **Distributed**, and **Release**.

A **Focal** completion gathers around one specially important place, thing, source, choice, point, or whole.

A **Distributed** completion needs a spread, a plurality, a range, a sequence, or a system. One place by itself would not be enough.

A **Release** completion happens because something that had been controlling the unfinished situation stops controlling it.

Release is especially worth noticing.

It does not simply mean “move away.”

Imagine a student who is required to choose one fixed project topic. Then that rule is lifted and several topics become available. The new option appears because the old restriction no longer governs the choice.

Or imagine a wall that stops movement. A gate is cut through it. The old edge no longer controls where movement must end.

What changed in both cases is Opening: more becomes available.

What made that change enough is Release: the old restriction lost its hold.

These three ways of becoming enough are called **Completion Topologies**.

They answer a second question:

**What made the change complete enough?**

### A finished result can remain in different ways

There is still one more question.

After the change becomes complete enough, what is available afterward?

Build a little bridge from blocks.

When it is finished, you can point and say, “There is the bridge.”

That is one kind of remainder. The achieved thing itself now stands there as the result.

The theory calls that **Standing**.

But perhaps what matters about the bridge is that the two sides support the span. Remove the supporting relation and you no longer have the same completed bridge.

That is another kind of remainder. The result holds through something.

The theory calls that **Bearing**.

And perhaps what matters most is that a toy car can now cross the gap. The bridge has changed what can happen.

That is a third kind of remainder.

The theory calls that **Efficacy**.

Standing, Bearing, and Efficacy are called **Persistence Modes**.

They do not tell us how long something lasts. A bridge can stand for five minutes or fifty years. A rule can have an effect while written quietly on a page. A repaired machine can possess an ability even while switched off.

The question is not simply, “How long does it stay?”

The question is:

**In what way does the completed result remain available?**

### Three questions, one grammar

We now have the same three questions we met before, but we can hear them a little more clearly.

**What changed?**

There are six broad answers.

**What made that change enough?**

There are three broad answers.

**How does the completed result remain available?**

There are three broad answers.

Six times three times three gives fifty-four possible positions.

But fifty-four is not important because it is a large number.

The important thing is that we do not need fifty-four unrelated ideas.

We have three questions.

Each position answers all three.

Consider the robot with the battery the wrong way around.

What changed?

A fault was corrected. That is **Repair**.

What made the correction enough?

The wrong arrangement stopped governing the robot. That is **Release**.

What remains available?

The robot can move again. What remains is an achieved ability. That is **Efficacy**.

So we have:

**Repair + Release + Efficacy.**

Notice what happened.

We did not begin by memorizing the special name of a box in a table.

We followed the situation.

The three answers together told us what sort of completed event we were looking at.

That is why the theory calls the system a **grammar**.

A grammar is useful because a few recurring parts can combine into many different forms.

### The shape is not the name, and the name is not the example

This is easiest to see with another simple case.

Water runs along a channel.

A gate is lowered at one point.

The water stops there.

What changed?

A difference became important at one place. Before the gate mattered, the water could continue. Now this point is different from the points before it.

That is Differentiation.

What made it enough?

One place became decisive.

That is Focal.

What remains?

The difference changes what the water can do.

That is Efficacy.

The theory can therefore describe the general shape as a **focal operative distinction**.

The Atlas gives a memorable name to one especially clear version of that shape: **Boundary act**.

And the channel gate is one concrete example.

A finish line can show the same shape.

A marked point where a pencil line must stop can show it too.

The channel is not the theory.

The words “Boundary act” are not the whole theory either.

First there is the structural shape made by the three answers. Then there can be a useful stable name. Then there can be many actual cases.

The mature theory calls these three levels:

**coordinate-generated form → canonical crystallization → concrete manifestation.**

The words are long. The order is simple.

**shape → clear name → actual case.**

The long words become useful when we need to be exact about which level we mean.

### A clear answer can still be wrong

Return to the red ball.

Suppose you confidently point to the left cup.

You say, “That one.”

The ball is really under the middle cup.

Your answer is wrong.

But it is not unfinished in the same way as silence or confusion. It is clear enough for someone to say, “No, not that cup.”

This is why the theory separates **intelligibility** from **truth**.

Before we can judge many things as true or false, right or wrong, successful or unsuccessful, there has to be something definite enough to judge.

A bad plan can still be a plan.

A false story can still be a complete story.

A mistaken explanation can still be clear enough for us to find the mistake.

The theory is studying the earlier question:

How did this become definite enough to be taken up as the thing we are now judging?

That does not make truth unimportant.

It tells us that truth is a different question.

### Little completions can sit inside larger unfinished things

Suppose you are building a Lego castle.

You finish one tower.

The tower is finished enough to stand as a tower.

But the castle is not finished.

Nothing has gone wrong.

One completion can sit inside a larger unfinished situation.

The same thing can happen in a journey. You can finish one stage and still have farther to go.

It can happen in a plan. One decision can be settled while another remains open.

It can happen in a sentence. A group of words can already make a clear local unit while the whole sentence still owes us something more.

This matters because completion is not the same thing as “nothing more can ever happen.”

A completed thing can be used inside something larger.

It can later be reopened.

It can fail later.

It can become one part of a new problem.

Completion only tells us that one particular demand has been answered enough that it no longer controls the situation in the same way.

### The same form can appear in very different places

Take one simple form from the Atlas.

A crowded keyring is in front of you. One key is pointed out.

Many stars are overhead. One star is singled out.

Several faces are in a photograph. One face is indicated.

The objects are completely different.

Keys are not stars.

Stars are not people in photographs.

But the completed movement is recognizably similar.

There is a field of candidates.

A difference becomes decisive at one place.

One candidate stands as the one meant.

That is why the theory is not mainly a theory about keys, stars, photographs, bridges, roads, robots, or sentences.

Those things give us places to see the forms.

The theory is interested in the way an unfinished situation changes, becomes enough, and leaves a result available.

### What this system does — and what we should not yet ask it to prove

At this point we can say one thing firmly about the system and keep several larger questions open.

The firm internal claim is that the published system has a clear compositional structure.

There are six Transformation Patterns.

There are three Completion Topologies.

There are three Persistence Modes.

They can be combined into fifty-four positions.

Each position can be examined by asking the same three questions.

That gives us a disciplined way to describe different kinds of completed situations.

But there is a larger claim we should not quietly smuggle in.

We have not shown, simply by building the system, that every possible event in the world must fit one of these fifty-four positions.

We have not shown that every person’s mind must use these three questions.

We have not shown that two careful observers will always choose the same coordinate for every difficult case.

Those are further questions.

The system can be complete as a system before those larger questions have been answered.

That should sound familiar by now.

A theory, too, can have one thing settled while another thing remains open.

### One more step

The five-year-old version began with a very small thought:

an unfinished thing asks for something, and the right kind of event can make the asking stop.

That thought still holds.

We can now make it a little sharper.

The asking stops not because time has stopped, and not because nothing else can ever happen.

It stops because the particular unfinished demand has received enough of the right kind of answer.

And once we see that, a new question appears.

Two situations can undergo the same kind of change and still become complete in different ways.

So the next thing to understand is not only **what changed**.

It is how the very same change can become **enough** in more than one way.

# Draft: The Seven-Year-Old Edition

## The Same Change Can Become Enough in Different Ways

A locked door can be opened.

That sentence sounds simple because it gives us the beginning and the end at once. First the door is locked. Then it is open.

But suppose we ask what really changed.

Perhaps one correct key was found among many nearly identical keys. Perhaps a new passage was made around the door. Perhaps a rule that required the door to stay locked was removed. Each situation ends with more access than before, but the route to enoughness is different.

That difference matters.

The last essay sharpened one idea: a situation is not complete merely because activity has stopped. It becomes complete when the particular unfinished demand has received enough of the right kind of answer.

Now we can ask a harder question.

If two situations need the same broad kind of change, can that change become enough in more than one way?

The answer is yes.

And that is why the theory needs three questions rather than one.

### The unfinished problem already limits the possible answer

Begin again with a ring of keys and a locked door.

The door is not asking for just anything.

It does not need a song, a colour, or a story.

For the purpose at hand, the unfinished part is access.

The situation is leaning toward a change that makes passage possible.

That leaning is what the theory calls **opening pressure**.

Opening pressure does not tell us every detail of the answer. It does not tell us which key will work, whether the lock will be removed, or whether another route will be found. But it tells us something important about what would count as relevant.

A broken wheel has a different pressure. A pile of parts has another. A live choice has another. Two things that cannot yet be told apart have another.

This is why the first question remains:

**What kind of unfinishedness changed?**

The theory gives six broad answers.

### Six kinds of change, seen more carefully

**Differentiation** happens when a difference becomes important enough to count.

A crowded keyring contains many visible differences already. The keys may have different scratches, teeth, colours, or sizes. But if the question is “Which one opens this lock?”, the useful difference has not yet become operative.

Then one key is identified.

That is not just “something changed.” A relevant difference has been made clear.

**Opening** happens when availability, reach, continuation, or possibility increases.

The main road is blocked. A side road makes another way through.

A wall stops movement. A gate is cut.

A student has only one permitted project topic. The restriction is lifted and several genuine choices become available.

**Relating** happens when a bearing between things becomes part of what the situation is.

A sensor is connected to an alarm.

A caller receives an answer.

Recipe steps are placed in sequence.

Two dancers begin adjusting to each other.

The relation is no longer optional background. It now helps constitute the completed situation.

**Repair** happens when a fault that had been governing the arrangement no longer governs it.

The robot battery is turned the right way.

The brake caliper is adjusted.

A mistaken arrangement is corrected enough that the failure stops controlling what happens.

**Integration** happens when parts acquire roles through a whole.

Loose bicycle parts become a bicycle.

Lego bricks become a castle.

Separate components stop being merely adjacent and become organized as parts of something larger.

**Settlement** happens when a live demand for another step is discharged.

A decision is made.

A journey reaches the destination that had still been owed.

A question receives an answer clear enough that the same question no longer governs what happens next.

These six are still the **Transformation Patterns**.

But now we are ready to notice something the simple list does not tell us.

Knowing the Transformation Pattern does not yet tell us how the transformation became enough.

### One difference can become complete in three different ways

Take Differentiation.

Imagine several keys on a keyring.

Someone points to one.

“That one.”

The useful difference becomes concentrated on a single candidate. One key now stands as the key meant.

That is one way Differentiation can become complete.

Now imagine a workshop floor with many activities happening across it.

A boundary is marked around one large region, and a rule is applied throughout it: assembly happens here.

The completed distinction does not depend on one tiny focal point. The criterion has to hold across an extent.

That is another way Differentiation can become complete.

Now imagine two arguments that have been blended together as though they said the same thing.

Someone carefully separates them.

The old conflation no longer controls the discussion. Two opposed positions can now stand independently.

That is a third way Differentiation can become complete.

The broad change is the same in all three cases:

a difference becomes operative.

But the condition that makes the change sufficient is different.

The theory calls these three conditions **Focal**, **Distributed**, and **Release**.

### Focal: enough gathers around one privileged place

A completion is **Focal** when one place, thing, source, target, choice, pivot, or whole has a privileged role in making the change enough.

The keyring is a clear example.

Many candidates exist, but one key is singled out.

The completed distinction gathers around that candidate.

Opening can also be Focal.

A dark room contains very little visible. One lamp switches on. A visible field opens from that source.

Relating can be Focal.

A loose rope is tied directly between two boats. The relation gathers at that connection.

Settlement can be Focal.

Several options remain live, and one is chosen.

Focal does not mean physically small.

A whole organization could become the focal centre of a completion.

The important thing is that the completed event is asymmetrically organized around one privileged locus.

The technical word “asymmetrically” adds little here. The structural point is simpler:

**one place matters in a special way.**

### Distributed: one place would not be enough

A completion is **Distributed** when a plurality, range, sequence, span, or system belongs to what makes the change sufficient.

Imagine mixed recyclables moving along a belt.

If the task is to separate plastic from everything else, pointing to one plastic bottle does not complete the job.

The distinguishing rule has to be carried across the field.

Or imagine a village on the far side of a river.

A bridge makes the village reachable because a connection spans the gap. No single point of the bridge is enough by itself. The span belongs to the achieved reach.

Or imagine recipe steps in the wrong order.

One step does not create the sequence.

The relation has to organize several members.

Distributed does not simply mean “many things happened.”

Many things can happen around one focal completion.

Distributed means that the plurality itself is part of what makes the result complete.

### Release: enough happens because an old governor loses its hold

**Release** is the easiest of the three to misunderstand.

It can sound like simple movement away from something.

But the deeper idea is not direction.

It is loss of control.

A completion happens by Release when something that had been holding the situation in its unfinished organization no longer holds it that way.

Imagine a student who is permitted only one project topic.

The restriction is lifted.

Now other topics stand as real options.

The student has not necessarily moved anywhere.

The important change is that the old rule no longer governs the field of choice.

Imagine two positions that have been treated as one.

They are separated.

The old conflation no longer governs how they must be understood.

Imagine a robot that cannot work because the battery is backwards.

The battery is corrected.

The old faulty arrangement no longer governs the robot’s functioning.

In each case, completion comes because a prior constraint loses its hold.

This is why “Release” is more exact than simply saying “away.”

The new situation is defined by what the old governor can no longer force.

### Now the first two questions can be heard separately

We can state the distinction plainly.

**Transformation Pattern asks what kind of change happened.**

**Completion Topology asks what made that change enough.**

They are related, but they are not the same question.

Differentiation can complete focally, distributively, or by release.

Opening can complete focally, distributively, or by release.

Relating can do the same.

So can Repair, Integration, and Settlement.

That gives us a useful test.

If we can keep the kind of change the same while changing how it becomes sufficient, then the two ideas are doing different jobs.

We do not yet need to make a grand claim about what this proves about the mind or the world.

For now, it is enough to see that the grammar itself needs both distinctions.

### There is still a third question

Suppose the change has happened.

Suppose we also know what made it enough.

We still have not said what kind of achieved result remains.

Return to the little block bridge.

When it is complete, we can simply point to it:

“There is the bridge.”

The achieved determination itself is available.

That is **Standing**.

But perhaps what matters is that the span remains held by the supports.

The bridge is what it is through those relations.

That is **Bearing**.

Or perhaps the important result is that the toy car can now cross.

The completion persists as a changed ability.

That is **Efficacy**.

These are the three **Persistence Modes**.

Standing, Bearing, and Efficacy answer:

**In what way does the completed result remain available?**

Again, this is different from the other two questions.

The bridge can involve Integration as its Transformation Pattern.

Its completion can be Focal if one whole becomes the relevant organized centre, or Distributed if the system of interdependent roles is what makes the integration enough.

And its persistence can still be Standing, Bearing, or Efficacy depending on what kind of achieved reality is central.

The questions can be combined because they are not simply three names for the same feature.

### Fifty-four positions are made from recurring roles

The arithmetic remains the same.

Six Transformation Patterns.

Three Completion Topologies.

Three Persistence Modes.

Six times three times three gives fifty-four.

But the number should now feel less like a surprising fact and more like a consequence of the structure.

Choose one kind of change.

Choose one way that change becomes enough.

Choose one way the result remains available.

The three together give one position.

Take:

**Differentiation + Focal + Standing.**

A field contains several possible candidates.

One is singled out.

That candidate remains as the distinguished one.

On a keyring, one key is pointed out.

In the night sky, one star is singled out.

In a photograph, one face is indicated.

Different materials. Same broad completed movement.

Now change only the final answer:

**Differentiation + Focal + Bearing.**

Two alternatives remain live.

One is selected.

But the rejected alternative still helps define what the chosen one means.

The left route is chosen rather than the right.

One switch setting is selected against the other.

One finalist wins against the other.

The change remains Differentiation.

The topology remains Focal.

What changes is how the result remains available.

It is not simply an isolated standing. It bears its contrast.

Now change the final answer again:

**Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy.**

A difference at one point changes what can happen.

A gate stops water.

A finish line ends the run.

A marked point terminates the pencil stroke.

Again, the first two answers stay the same.

The third changes.

This gives us our first clear glimpse of why the grammar can have many positions without becoming a pile of unrelated labels.

The same contributions recur in different combinations.

### A coordinate gives a shape before it gives us a name

We can now build one form without beginning from its canonical title.

Suppose we choose:

**Opening + Release + Standing.**

What would that require?

Opening tells us that availability must increase.

Release tells us that the increase becomes enough because an old restriction loses its hold.

Standing tells us that the newly available possibility itself remains as the achieved result.

Now look for a case.

A hiker is required to follow one marked trail.

The restriction is lifted.

A second route becomes genuinely available.

The result is an option that now stands.

The Atlas gives a clear canonical name to this: **Released option**.

But notice the order.

We could understand the broad event before hearing the name.

The coordinate had already told us what kind of completion to look for.

That broad event-shape is the **coordinate-generated form**.

The selected stable name is the **canonical crystallization**.

The hiker and trail are a **concrete manifestation**.

At an earlier level we used the simpler sequence:

**shape → name → example.**

That remains correct.

We can now add one more detail:

the shape is not chosen freely. It is constrained by the three answers.

### The same shape can survive a change of material

Take **Opening + Distributed + Bearing**.

The general event is this:

something previously local becomes reachable across a span because a connection maintains that reach.

A village across a river becomes reachable through a bridge.

A computer far from a router remains connected through a cable.

Two cliffs become linked by a suspension bridge.

The materials differ.

But the completion has the same broad organization.

This is important because it helps us see what the theory is trying to study.

It is not making a list of things such as roads, bridges, cables, keys, students, robots, sentences, and choices.

It is asking whether completed events can share a structural form even when their contents differ.

That is also why subject matter alone cannot tell us the coordinate.

A bridge might matter because it opens reach.

Or because its parts become an integrated whole.

Or because it relates two banks.

The object “bridge” does not decide the form.

The unresolved demand and the completed organization do.

### A wrong but clear answer still gives us something to judge

The separation between intelligibility and truth also becomes sharper at this level.

Suppose two routes remain possible.

You choose the left route because you believe it reaches the destination.

It does not.

Your choice may be wrong.

But the completed structure of the decision can still be clear.

One alternative was selected against another.

The fact that the choice later proves mistaken does not mean there was never a determinate choice.

Or suppose a doctor gives a diagnosis that later turns out to be false.

The diagnosis can still be complete enough to understand, test, criticize, and reject.

The theory therefore asks a question that comes before endorsement:

**How did this become definite enough to count as the thing we are now judging?**

Truth matters.

Correctness matters.

Success matters.

But they answer later questions.

### The path to completion is not always the completed form

There is another distinction beginning to appear.

Imagine building a Lego castle.

One tower is finished.

Then another.

A wall is rebuilt because the first version falls over.

The gate is added last.

The history contains several local completions, mistakes, corrections, and changes.

At the end, the castle stands as an integrated whole.

The completed form does not tell us every step of the history.

A similar thing happens in sentences.

A group of words can make sense locally.

Later words can show that the earlier part belongs inside a larger structure.

Sometimes we even have to revise what we first thought the sentence was doing.

So there are at least two ways to describe a completed event.

We can ask:

**What completed organization was achieved?**

And we can ask:

**How did the situation get there?**

Those questions are connected.

But just as Transformation Pattern and Completion Topology should not be collapsed, the finished structural description should not be mistaken for the whole history that produced it.

We will need more language before we can describe that distinction fully.

For now, it is enough to keep the two views apart.

### What the system has shown so far

We can say more now than we could before.

The system is not merely a set of interesting examples.

Its three questions make different contributions.

We can keep one answer fixed while varying another and still recognize what has remained the same.

We can use the three answers to anticipate the broad shape of a completed event before hearing its familiar canonical name.

And we can see the same completed shape appearing in different subject matters.

Those are substantive achievements inside the published system.

But we should still be careful about what follows from them.

The fact that the grammar can distinguish these roles internally does not yet prove that the human mind contains three separate mental boxes.

The fact that the Atlas has fifty-four positions does not yet prove that no other kind of completed form is possible.

The fact that we can classify familiar examples does not yet prove that every careful observer will classify every difficult example in the same way.

Those are larger questions.

A theory becomes stronger not by pretending those questions are already answered, but by making clear what would still need to be shown.

### The next pressure

We started with one idea:

an unfinished situation becomes complete when its particular demand receives enough of the right kind of answer.

We then separated three questions:

**What changed?**

**What made that change enough?**

**How does the result remain available?**

Now another pattern has become visible.

The same answer can reappear across very different examples.

The same Transformation Pattern can survive changes in topology and persistence.

The same topology can appear under different transformations.

The same Persistence Mode can recur in situations that have almost nothing else in common.

So the next task is to learn how to notice what stays constant when the material changes.

That is where a list of examples begins to turn into a structure.

## The Same Shape Can Appear in Different Things

A key is not a star.

A star is not a face in a photograph.

Yet the theory can describe a completed event involving each of them in the same way.

There is a crowded keyring. One key is pointed out.

There are many stars overhead. One star is singled out.

There are several faces in a photograph. One face is indicated.

The materials are different. The event-shape is similar.

In each case, a field contains several candidates. A difference becomes decisive at one place. One candidate remains as the one meant.

The same three questions now let us notice something that the individual examples concealed.

So far we have learned that an unfinished situation can already have a particular demand, and that three different questions help describe how it becomes complete:

**What changed?**

**What made that change enough?**

**How does the completed result remain available?**

Now we can begin asking a new question:

**What stays the same when the subject matter changes?**

That question is one of the ways a theory begins to move beyond examples.

### Opening pressure is not tied to one kind of object

Consider three situations.

A ring of keys contains several possible candidates, but you do not know which one opens the lock.

A road is blocked, so your planned route cannot continue.

A bicycle brake rubs against the wheel and prevents the bicycle from working properly.

These situations involve different objects.

But each is unfinished in a particular way.

The keyring lacks the operative difference that tells you which key matters.

The road lacks available continuation.

The bicycle has a fault that still governs its operation.

The theory calls this structured unfinishedness **opening pressure**.

At this level we can begin to see opening pressure not just as “something is still needed,” but as a recurring kind of unresolved demand.

Different contents can have the same kind of pressure.

A photograph and a keyring can both contain a missing relevant difference.

A road and a computer network can both contain restricted reach.

A robot and a bicycle can both contain a governing fault.

A pile of Lego pieces and a box of bicycle parts can both contain unorganized plurality.

A travel decision and a choice of project topic can both contain a live demand for settlement.

The thing that is unfinished is not determined by what the objects are called.

It is determined by what the situation still owes.

### Six transformations can recur across domains

This makes the six **Transformation Patterns** easier to understand.

They are not six kinds of object.

They are six kinds of decisive change.

**Differentiation** appears whenever a difference that was not yet operative becomes important enough to matter.

A key is selected.

A star is singled out.

A group of mixed seeds is sorted.

A discussion separates two positions that had been blurred together.

The materials differ. The change has the same broad form.

**Opening** appears whenever availability, reach, continuation, or possibility increases relative to a prior restriction.

A side road gives another way through.

A bridge makes the far bank reachable.

A lamp makes a visible field available.

A restriction is lifted and another option becomes real.

**Relating** appears whenever a bearing among things becomes constitutive of the completed situation.

A rope connects two boats.

Recipe steps are put in order.

Two rowers adjust to each other.

A sensor is connected to an alarm.

**Repair** appears whenever a failing organization is changed so the fault no longer governs the result.

A battery is turned.

A brake is aligned.

A mistaken arrangement is corrected.

A defective relation is replaced by one that satisfies the operative criterion.

**Integration** appears whenever separate parts gain roles through a larger whole.

Loose bicycle parts become a bicycle.

Lego bricks become a castle.

Separate components become a working system.

**Settlement** appears whenever a live demand for another step is discharged.

A choice is made.

A destination is reached.

An explanation becomes complete enough that the same question no longer governs the discussion.

The important gain here is not memorizing more examples.

It is seeing that examples can change while the structural role remains recognizable.

### The topology also survives changes of subject matter

Now look at **Focal** completion.

One key is singled out.

One lamp becomes the source from which a field opens.

One direct connection ties two boats.

One option is selected.

These examples belong to different Transformation Patterns.

Yet something about the completion is similar.

One locus has a privileged role in making the event enough.

We can say the same thing about **Distributed** completion.

A criterion is applied across a field of recyclables.

A bridge maintains reach across a span.

Recipe steps are organized across a sequence.

A system of parts depends on many roles working together.

No single point is sufficient by itself.

The plurality belongs to the completed structure.

And **Release** can recur too.

A conflation no longer forces two positions to count as one.

A route restriction is lifted.

A faulty arrangement stops governing a machine.

A live demand no longer forces another decision.

The subject matter changes.

The completion still happens through loss of prior governance.

This is why Completion Topology is not just a description of where an object sits.

It is a description of what makes the change sufficient.

### Persistence can be seen by changing what we attend to

The same bridge can help us see another kind of recurrence.

Imagine a suspension bridge between two cliffs.

If we point to the finished bridge itself, we are interested in **Standing**.

If we care that the far side remains reachable because the span maintains the connection, we are interested in **Bearing**.

If we care that people can now cross, we are interested in **Efficacy**.

The bridge has not changed.

Our question has.

This helps us avoid a mistake.

Standing, Bearing, and Efficacy are not necessarily labels permanently attached to objects.

They describe the mode in which a completed result is being taken up.

A rule written on a page can stand as the settled rule.

It can bear through the relation between a condition and the cases to which it applies.

It can be efficacious because it changes what actions are permitted.

A repaired machine can stand as repaired.

Its correct operation can depend on relations among parts.

Its repair can remain as recovered capacity.

The theory therefore asks us to distinguish the object from the structure of completion we are describing.

### The fifty-four positions form a space of possible descriptions

We still have six Transformation Patterns, three Completion Topologies, and three Persistence Modes.

The arithmetic still yields fifty-four positions.

But now we can understand those positions in a better way.

They are not fifty-four shelves, each containing one special kind of object.

They are fifty-four combinations of recurring structural contributions.

Consider the first three positions in one small family.

**Differentiation + Focal + Standing**

One candidate becomes distinguished and remains as the one indicated.

Key. Star. Face.

**Differentiation + Focal + Bearing**

One alternative becomes distinguished, but the rejected alternative remains part of what the chosen one means.

Left route rather than right.

One switch position rather than the other.

One finalist rather than the other.

**Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy**

A difference at one place changes what can happen.

A channel gate stops flow.

A finish line ends the run.

A marked point terminates the pencil stroke.

The materials change even within each position.

The first two coordinates stay fixed across the three positions.

The third changes.

This shows us something important.

The positions have internal neighborhoods.

They can be compared systematically.

We are no longer merely saying, “Here are three examples that seem interesting.”

We are asking what changes when one part of the coordinate changes.

### A generated form is what remains before we choose the material

Take **Opening + Distributed + Bearing**.

A bridge crosses a river.

A cable connects a remote computer.

A suspension bridge connects two cliffs.

If we remove river, computer, cable, and cliffs, what is left?

Something like this:

**Something previously local becomes available at a separated position because a connection maintains reach across the intervening span.**

That is more abstract than any one example.

But it is not empty.

It still says:

- availability increased;
- the span belongs to what made the opening enough;
- the achieved reach remains through the connection.

The theory calls this the **coordinate-generated form**.

The Atlas gives a stable, more specific name: **Span-carried reach**.

And then the bridge, cable, and suspension bridge are **concrete manifestations**.

So the three levels are becoming easier to keep apart.

The generated form is the broad structural event required by the coordinate.

The canonical crystallization is a clear semantic realization chosen as the stable Atlas form.

The manifestation is the event happening in particular material.

At an earlier level, “shape → name → example” was enough.

Now we can see why the distinction matters.

If we confuse the manifestation with the form, we might think the theory is about bridges.

If we confuse the canonical name with the form, we might think the label contains everything the coordinate requires.

Neither is true.

### One canonical name can be narrower than the generated form

The channel gate, finish line, and pencil mark belong to:

**Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy.**

The coordinate requires a difference concentrated at one place that changes what can continue or occur.

The Atlas calls one especially clear realization **Boundary act**.

That name fits the examples well.

But the coordinate does not force every possible instance to be a literal boundary.

What the coordinate forces is broader:

a focal difference becomes operative.

This means a coordinate can be structurally clear while still allowing more than one semantic realization.

We do not yet need a full theory of how wide or narrow that freedom can be.

It is enough to notice that “the coordinate determines the structure” does not always mean “the coordinate determines one and only one familiar name.”

This will matter later.

### The history of a completion can vary too

Now compare two events that end with a similar completed structure.

A side road is already marked on the map. When the main road is blocked, you simply take the alternate route.

In another case, there is no obvious alternate route. You try one road, discover another blockage, turn back, ask for directions, and finally find a path through.

The completed result may still be an available alternate passage.

But the histories differ.

One was direct.

One involved failed attempts and reorientation.

This shows that a completed form can remain recognizable even when the path to it changes.

Sentences make this especially easy to see.

Sometimes a sentence unfolds exactly as expected.

Sometimes an early phrase suggests one organization and later material forces us to revise it.

The final completed structure can be clear even though the route to that structure included provisional interpretations.

So we should keep two questions apart:

**What completed form was achieved?**

and

**What history produced it?**

The mature theory will need language for that history.

For now, the distinction itself is enough.

### Intelligibility can recur even when truth changes

Suppose three people give three different explanations for the same event.

One explanation is correct.

One is partly correct.

One is completely wrong.

All three may still be clear enough to understand.

Each may have a determinate structure.

This means the same broad form of completed intelligibility can appear in claims with different truth values.

The theory therefore cannot use truth as the rule that defines completion.

If it did, false but perfectly understandable statements would become impossible to describe as complete.

Instead, the theory asks whether the situation has become determinate enough for a stable next uptake.

That next uptake might be agreement.

It might be rejection.

It might be correction.

It might be use.

It might be another question.

The important point is that the earlier unresolved demand is no longer controlling the situation in the same way.

### The theory is about completion structure, not subject matter

At this stage we can state the object of the theory more clearly.

A bridge, a diagnosis, a sentence, a route, a machine, a choice, and a photograph have almost nothing in common as subject matters.

Yet they can instantiate similar forms of completion.

Conversely, the same subject matter can support different forms.

A bridge can be described as an Opening because it makes reach possible.

It can be described as Integration because parts become a functioning whole.

It can be described as Relating because two banks acquire a constitutive connection.

The word “bridge” does not decide the coordinate.

The structural transition does.

This is one of the reasons the theory aims to be domain-general.

But “aims to be” is the careful phrase.

Seeing recurrence across examples in the corpus is not the same thing as proving that the same structure will recur everywhere.

### What we can claim, and what still remains open

The internal architecture is becoming stronger.

We can identify recurring contributions.

We can compare neighboring coordinates.

We can abstract a generated form away from its material.

We can distinguish generated form, canonical crystallization, and concrete manifestation.

We can see that the same broad form may arise through different histories.

These achievements make the system more than a loose list.

But they still do not prove everything we might hope to know.

We have not yet shown that every possible domain will exhibit the same patterns.

We have not shown that every difficult case has exactly one correct coordinate.

We have not shown that the three dimensions correspond to separate psychological mechanisms.

We have not shown that a simpler system could not someday explain the same observations equally well.

A theory should not hide those openings.

It should know which parts of itself are complete and which remain research questions.

### What stays the same?

We began by comparing a key, a star, and a face in a photograph.

The objects differ completely.

What remains is a structural movement:

many candidates;

one difference becomes decisive;

one candidate stands as the one meant.

Now we can do the same kind of comparison across topologies, persistence modes, generated forms, and formation histories.

That gives us a new tool.

Instead of asking only, “What is this example about?”, we can ask:

**Which part of the completed structure remains the same when the example changes?**

And once we learn to hold one part steady while changing another, the grammar begins to show its internal joints.

That is the next step.

## The difference between resemblance and structural sameness

There is another reason to keep using very different examples.

Two examples can look alike without sharing the same completed form.

Two roads may both be blocked, but one event may be an Opening because a new passage becomes available, while another may be Repair because a fault in the routing system is corrected.

Two bridges may look almost identical, but one may matter because it spans a gap, another because its parts become integrated into a working whole, and another because it creates an operative relation between two previously separated banks.

So visual or semantic resemblance is not enough.

The theory asks for structural sameness.

That means we need to compare what role the change plays.

This is a useful discipline because it prevents us from classifying by topic.

A “bridge example” is not automatically one kind of form.

A “choice example” is not automatically Settlement.

A “repair example” in ordinary language may actually be Opening or Relating if that is the governing transformation.

The completed structure has to be recovered from the unresolved demand and the resulting organization.

## A recurring example can teach several different lessons

Take the blocked road again.

At one level, it teaches Opening.

The route cannot continue, then another path becomes available.

At another level, it teaches Distributed completion if the alternate path works because a wider sequence of roads together makes passage possible.

At another level, it can teach Standing if the result is simply that another route now exists.

It can teach Bearing if the route remains available through its maintained connection to the larger network.

It can teach Efficacy if the important result is that travel can now continue.

The point is not that every description is equally correct at once.

The point is that the same material can support different structural questions.

That makes the three-axis grammar useful.

It forces us to say what aspect of the completed event we are actually describing.

## The first real abstraction

At this stage the reader should begin to feel that abstraction does not mean making examples vague.

It means removing details that do not matter to the structural comparison while keeping the details that do.

If we remove too little, the examples stay trapped in their domains.

If we remove too much, nothing useful remains.

The grammar gives a rule for how much to keep.

Keep:
- the kind of unresolvedness;
- the decisive change;
- the condition of sufficiency;
- the mode of achieved availability.

Let the rest vary.

That is how a bridge and a network cable can become comparable without pretending they are the same object.

## Why this matters for the whole theory

The theory only becomes domain-general if the structural form survives changes in material.

That does not mean the theory has already been validated across all domains.

It means the architecture is designed to make cross-domain comparison possible.

The stronger claim would require held-out evidence later.

At this stage the modest claim is enough:

the same coordinate vocabulary can be used coherently across very different examples in the corpus.

That is a real internal achievement.

# Draft: The Nine-Year-Old Edition

## What Stays the Same When One Part Changes?

Suppose we have three completed events.

In the first, one key is pointed out from a crowded keyring.

In the second, one route is chosen rather than another.

In the third, a finish line marks where a run ends.

The examples are different, but they are not unrelated.

All three involve **Differentiation**.

All three are **Focal**.

What changes is the way the result remains available.

In the keyring case, one candidate simply stands as the one meant.

In the route case, the chosen route still bears its relation to the rejected alternative.

In the finish-line case, the distinction changes what can happen next.

So the three positions are:

**Differentiation + Focal + Standing**

**Differentiation + Focal + Bearing**

**Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy**

This is the kind of comparison that begins to reveal the grammar from the inside.

We are no longer only asking whether an example fits a position.

We are asking what happens when one contribution changes while the others remain the same.

### The opening pressure and the transformation belong together, but they are not identical

An unfinished situation has a pressure.

Something is still owed.

If several candidates cannot yet be told apart in the relevant way, the pressure concerns a missing difference.

If a route cannot continue, the pressure concerns restricted availability.

If two things do not yet bear on one another in the needed way, the pressure concerns a missing relation.

If an arrangement is failing, the pressure concerns a governing fault.

If parts lack organization through a whole, the pressure concerns unorganized plurality.

If a demand for another step remains live, the pressure concerns something still unsettled.

These pressures correspond naturally to the six Transformation Patterns:

- Differentiation
- Opening
- Relating
- Repair
- Integration
- Settlement

But the pressure is not yet the transformation.

The pressure describes what is missing.

The transformation describes the decisive change that answers that missingness.

A locked door can contain an Opening pressure before any opening has occurred.

A faulty machine can contain a Repair pressure before anything is repaired.

A live choice can contain a Settlement pressure before any settlement happens.

So the grammar already contains a before and an after.

The opening pressure is the shaped incompletion.

The Transformation Pattern is the kind of decisive change that answers it.

### Hold the transformation fixed

Now keep one Transformation Pattern the same and vary the other two coordinates.

Take **Opening**.

**Opening + Focal + Standing**

A source becomes the place from which an available field stands.

A lamp opens visibility from one point.

A beacon gives a field a usable origin.

**Opening + Distributed + Standing**

An alternate route becomes available through a wider field.

A blocked road is bypassed by a side road.

A closed corridor is bypassed by another path through the building.

**Opening + Release + Standing**

An old restriction stops governing the available choices.

A second project topic becomes genuinely selectable.

A machine locked to one mode becomes able to enter another mode.

In all three cases, availability increases.

That is the invariant contribution of Opening.

But the way sufficiency is achieved differs:

- around one privileged locus;
- through a plurality or span;
- through loss of prior restriction.

And if we then change Standing to Bearing or Efficacy, the kind of achieved remainder changes too.

This is what it means to begin treating the dimensions as separate contributions.

### Hold the topology fixed

Now hold **Distributed** constant.

Differentiation + Distributed means a relevant difference must hold across a plurality or extent.

Opening + Distributed means increased availability requires a span, range, or distributed field.

Relating + Distributed means the relation itself depends on several positions, members, or moments.

Repair + Distributed means correction must be achieved through a larger arrangement rather than one focal change alone.

Integration + Distributed means the whole depends on interdependent roles across parts.

Settlement + Distributed means resolution depends on conditions, cases, steps, or a field rather than one single settlement point.

The Transformation Pattern changes.

The topology remains Distributed.

What survives across the examples is not a common object or subject matter.

It is the role of plurality in making the change sufficient.

One place by itself would not do the work.

### Hold the Persistence Mode fixed

Now hold **Bearing** constant.

A selected route can bear its contrast against the rejected route.

A remote connection can bear through a span.

A balanced system can bear through counteracting relations.

An integrated whole can bear through dependencies among parts.

A settlement can bear through a condition or explanatory relation.

Again, the subject matter changes.

The topology may change.

The transformation may change.

But Bearing contributes a familiar kind of remainder:

the result remains what it is through some constitutive relation.

This is why Standing, Bearing, and Efficacy are not merely three descriptions we happen to like.

Within the grammar, they repeatedly change the kind of post-completion availability while the other contributions can stay fixed.

### What “independent” can mean here

It is tempting to say that the three dimensions are independent.

That word can mean several things, so we should be careful.

At this stage, the theory can make a modest but useful claim.

A Transformation Pattern can recur under different topologies and persistence modes.

A Completion Topology can recur under different transformations and persistence modes.

A Persistence Mode can recur under different transformations and topologies.

Each contribution remains recognizable when the others are changed.

That is a form of independence **inside the grammar**.

It does not yet prove that the brain stores these dimensions separately.

It does not prove that they are statistically independent in experiments.

It does not prove that no deeper theory could someday reduce them to something else.

The claim is narrower:

they do different functional work in the present architecture.

That is enough to support composition.

### Why composition matters

If three contributions can be varied separately, their combinations can carry structure.

Consider:

**Relating + Distributed + Bearing**

Relating says that a bearing among terms becomes constitutive.

Distributed says that the relation depends on a plurality; no single locus is enough.

Bearing says that the completed result remains through mutual or relational support.

Now look at two dancers adjusting to each other.

Neither dancer alone is the relation.

The completed organization exists across both.

And each remains intelligible through the other.

That is why the Atlas crystallizes this region as **Reciprocal shift**.

The coordinate does not merely label the result after the fact.

The three values already narrow what kind of event could belong there.

### Generated form becomes more than a slogan

We can now state the logic more carefully.

Take any coordinate:

**Transformation Pattern + Completion Topology + Persistence Mode**

The first part constrains what kind of unresolvedness must change.

The second constrains what makes that change sufficient.

The third constrains what kind of achieved result remains.

The conjunction of those three constraints gives a **coordinate-generated form**.

It is still possible that several semantic realizations fit that form.

But not anything will fit.

The coordinate already excludes many kinds of event.

For example:

**Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy**

must involve:

- a relevant difference;
- concentrated at one privileged locus;
- becoming operative in what can happen.

That is already enough to explain why a channel gate, finish line, and marked endpoint belong together structurally.

The canonical name **Boundary act** is helpful.

But the coordinate has done explanatory work before the name arrived.

### The 54 positions are not equally different from one another

A table with fifty-four cells can look like fifty-four separate boxes.

But once we compare neighboring cells, the space becomes more structured.

Some positions differ only in Persistence Mode.

Some differ only in Completion Topology.

Some differ only in Transformation Pattern.

This creates families and neighborhoods.

The first nine positions can all be read as different conjugations of Differentiation.

The next nine can be read as different conjugations of Opening.

And so on.

We are not yet ready to describe the full periodic structure of the Atlas.

But we can already see that there are patterned substitutions.

This matters because a grammar is more than a set.

A set can contain many unrelated items.

A grammar contains recurring roles whose recombination changes form in systematic ways.

### The canonical crystallization can be checked against the coordinate

Because the coordinate already generates structure, the canonical name can now be evaluated.

Take **Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy**.

The Atlas gives **Boundary act**.

Does that fit?

Yes.

A boundary is a focal difference.

Its importance is operative: it changes what may continue.

But is “boundary” absolutely forced by the coordinate?

Not necessarily.

The coordinate requires a focal operative distinction.

A boundary is one especially lucid realization.

This means the canonical crystallization should be understood as a stable semantic choice inside the generated region.

It can be very tightly guided without being identical to the bare coordinate.

That distinction protects the theory from saying more than the coordinate actually warrants.

### Manifestations can support the form without defining it

The same caution applies to examples.

A gate in a channel is not the definition of Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy.

Neither is a finish line.

Neither is a pencil mark.

They are manifestations.

They help us recognize the event-shape.

But if we use them as definitions, we make the theory too narrow.

A strong theory should be able to say what the form requires even when every familiar example is removed.

Examples provide illustration and corpus-internal support.

They are not the deepest theoretical object.

### A history can vary while the completed form stays comparable

The same kind of substitution appears in formation history.

Suppose two people end with the same selected route.

One looked at a map and chose immediately.

The other tried a road, encountered a blockage, backtracked, asked for directions, and finally chose the same route.

The completed decision can be structurally similar.

The histories are not.

This tells us that the coordinate is not a complete record of everything that happened.

The coordinate describes the achieved organization.

The history describes the route by which that organization became governing.

Later we will need a more exact vocabulary for this historical layer.

For now, the distinction is enough:

same completed form does not require same history.

### Intelligibility is another invariant under changing truth value

A similar comparison can be made with truth.

A diagnosis can be true.

Another diagnosis can be false.

Both can be structurally complete enough to understand.

A plan can be wise or foolish.

Both can be complete as plans.

A fictional event can be impossible in reality but fully organized in the story.

So truth value can change while completed intelligibility remains.

This does not mean truth is unimportant.

It means the structural conditions for something becoming judgeable are not identical with the later judgment.

Again, one contribution can vary while another remains.

The habit of holding one thing fixed and changing another is becoming central.

### What kind of theory is beginning to appear?

The theory now looks less like a catalogue and more like a compositional system.

It starts with structured incompletion.

It identifies a decisive kind of change.

It identifies the topology of sufficiency.

It identifies the mode of post-completion availability.

Those contributions can be recombined.

The resulting coordinates constrain local forms.

Canonical names sharpen those forms semantically.

Concrete manifestations realize them in particular materials.

Formation history explains how the completed organization emerged.

Truth, correctness, and success remain later evaluations.

That is already a layered theory.

We do not yet need every mature term.

But the architecture is beginning to show itself.

### The claims boundary becomes clearer too

The more systematic the internal structure becomes, the easier it is to overclaim.

We should resist that.

Showing that the three axes contribute separately inside the grammar does not prove that they are separate mental modules.

Showing that the 54 positions are systematically generated does not prove that the space is exhaustive.

Showing that the same form can recur across several examples does not prove that it will generalize to every domain.

Showing that a canonical crystallization fits its coordinate does not prove that no other crystallization could fit as well.

Internal structure is evidence about the coherence of the theory.

External reality requires further evidence.

Keeping those claims separate is not a weakness.

It is part of what makes the theory intelligible as a research programme.

### What the next step requires

We can now do something we could not do at the beginning.

We can hold one axis fixed.

We can vary another.

We can compare neighboring coordinates.

We can ask what the coordinate itself requires before we look at its familiar example.

That changes the status of the Atlas.

It is beginning to look less like a table of discovered categories and more like the output of a compositional rule.

The next question is therefore unavoidable:

If the coordinates themselves constrain the kind of event that belongs at a position, can we treat the system as genuinely generative rather than merely classificatory?

That is the next threshold.

## A useful part of a system should matter when it changes

There is a simple test we can apply to the architecture.

If changing one coordinate value while keeping the others fixed never changes the generated form, then that coordinate is not doing much work.

But when we change Standing to Bearing, the achieved result shifts from something that simply stands to something that remains through relation.

When we change Bearing to Efficacy, the remainder shifts toward capacity, operation, or consequence.

When we change Focal to Distributed, the condition of sufficiency shifts from one privileged locus to a constitutive plurality.

When we change Distributed to Release, the completion begins to depend on loss of prior governance.

And when we change Differentiation to Opening, the governing change itself shifts from operative difference to increased availability.

Each substitution alters something systematic.

That is why the three axes earn their place inside the grammar.

## Local neighborhoods in the Atlas

A coordinate becomes easier to understand when placed beside its nearest neighbors.

Consider:

**Opening + Distributed + Standing**

This is Alternate passage.

Now keep Opening and Distributed fixed, but change Standing to Bearing.

We reach Span-carried reach.

Change Bearing to Efficacy.

We reach Field distribution.

The three cells form a small neighborhood.

They share the same governing change and the same topology of sufficiency.

What changes is the mode of persistence.

The first leaves an available passage standing.

The second leaves reach maintained through a span.

The third leaves availability operating across a field.

This kind of neighborhood comparison can be repeated throughout the Atlas.

The table becomes easier to navigate because each move has a structural meaning.

## Internal separability versus empirical separability

The theory can therefore say:

the three axes are separable in representation.

We can vary them independently inside the grammar.

But empirical work may show stronger couplings.

Perhaps some Transformation Patterns occur much more often with certain topologies.

Perhaps people confuse Bearing and Efficacy in some domains.

Perhaps one dimension proves unnecessary under testing.

Those possibilities do not make the current architecture incoherent.

They identify what would have to be tested.

The important distinction is between:

**the theory can represent the dimensions separately**

and

**the world or cognition has been shown to instantiate them separately.**

Only the first is presently established internally.

# Draft: The Ten-Year-Old Edition

## Why This Is a Grammar and Not Merely a Table

Imagine being handed a table with fifty-four names in it.

You could memorize the names.

You could learn one example for each.

You could even become very good at matching familiar cases to familiar labels.

But that would not yet tell you why the table has the shape it does.

A classification tells us where things belong.

A grammar does something stronger.

It tells us how forms can be generated from recurring contributions.

That is the next step in the theory.

### Start with the unresolved field

Every completed event begins, in this framework, with a structured incompletion.

Something is still owed.

A relevant difference is missing.

Availability is restricted.

A relation has not yet become constitutive.

A fault governs the arrangement.

Parts lack organization through a whole.

A live demand remains unsettled.

This is **opening pressure**.

It matters because it constrains what kind of decisive change could answer the situation.

A missing difference calls for Differentiation.

Restricted availability calls for Opening.

Missing constitutive bearing calls for Relating.

Governed misfit calls for Repair.

Unorganized plurality calls for Integration.

Residual demand calls for Settlement.

This is the first constraint.

### Transformation Pattern is one grammatical contribution

The six Transformation Patterns are not topics.

They specify what kind of unresolvedness is altered between the opening field and the completed field.

That means each one contributes a structural role.

Differentiation says:

a relevant difference must become operative.

Opening says:

availability must increase.

Relating says:

a bearing among terms must become constitutive.

Repair says:

a fault must cease to govern.

Integration says:

parts must acquire roles through a whole.

Settlement says:

a live demand must be discharged.

These are not complete forms yet.

They are one contribution to a form.

### Completion Topology adds a second constraint

Now add the second question.

What makes the transformation sufficient?

If one privileged locus organizes the completion, the topology is **Focal**.

If a plurality, span, range, sequence, or system belongs to what makes the transformation enough, the topology is **Distributed**.

If completion occurs because a prior governor loses its hold, the topology is **Release**.

This changes the form substantially.

Opening by Focal concentration is not the same as Opening through a distributed span.

Opening through a distributed span is not the same as Opening through release from a restriction.

The Transformation Pattern is the same.

The topology changes what counts as enough.

### Persistence Mode adds the third constraint

Then ask what kind of result remains available.

If the achieved determination itself stands, the Persistence Mode is **Standing**.

If the result remains through a constitutive relation, condition, support, source, contrast, or frame, it is **Bearing**.

If the result remains as capacity, operation, consequence, or changed possibility, it is **Efficacy**.

Again, this is not decoration.

Changing the Persistence Mode changes the completed form.

A distinguished item that simply stands is different from a distinguished item whose identity bears through contrast.

Both differ from a distinction whose importance lies in what it now makes possible or impossible.

### Three constraints generate a local event-shape

Now we can state the compositional rule.

Choose a Transformation Pattern.

Choose a Completion Topology.

Choose a Persistence Mode.

The three values jointly constrain what kind of completed event can belong at that coordinate.

This is the core idea behind the phrase **constraint grammar**.

Take:

**Differentiation + Focal + Standing**

The coordinate requires:

- a relevant difference;
- concentrated around one privileged locus;
- leaving the achieved determination available as something that stands.

The keyring example fits.

The star example fits.

The photograph example fits.

But the coordinate was intelligible before any one of those examples was supplied.

It generated a local structural expectation.

That local expectation is what the theory calls the **coordinate-generated form**.

### The generated form can be written as a sentence

The theory often expresses the generated form as a compact event-sentence.

For Differentiation + Focal + Standing:

> A difference is concentrated upon one focal candidate until that candidate stands as the distinguished one.

That sentence is more than a paraphrase of “Focal mark.”

It exposes the roles contributed by the three axes.

- Differentiation supplies the difference.
- Focal supplies the concentrated locus.
- Standing supplies the achieved remainder.

The sentence is therefore generated by the coordinate.

This is why the mature theory uses the phrase **phenomenological syntax**.

The word “syntax” here does not mean ordinary word order.

It means that recurring functional roles combine systematically.

The word “phenomenological” means that the roles concern how a situation appears and becomes organized as unresolved, completed, held, operative, or released.

The resulting syntax is event-shaped.

It contains a before, a decisive change, a condition of sufficiency, and an achieved result.

### A second coordinate shows that the rule is reusable

Now take:

**Opening + Release + Standing**

Opening requires increased availability.

Release requires sufficiency through loss of prior restriction.

Standing requires the new possibility itself to remain available.

That gives a generated form like:

a prior restriction loses its governance so that an additional possibility stands as genuinely available.

Now look at manifestations:

a hiker gains another route when a restriction is lifted;

a student gains real topic choice when a fixed-topic rule is removed;

a machine gains another selectable mode when a lock is released.

The canonical crystallization is **Released option**.

Again, the coordinate generated the broad event before the name and examples arrived.

### A third coordinate shows semantic latitude

Take:

**Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy**

The coordinate requires a focal difference that changes what can happen.

A gate stops water.

A finish line ends the run.

A marked endpoint terminates a line.

The Atlas crystallizes this as **Boundary act**.

But notice something important.

The coordinate does not say that the operative difference must literally be a boundary.

“Boundary act” is a particularly clear semantic realization.

The generated form is broader:

**focal operative distinction**.

This tells us that generation does not always produce one uniquely forced name.

The grammar can strongly constrain a region while leaving some semantic latitude inside it.

That will matter more later.

### Why the canonical name comes after the generated form

If we begin from canonical names, the theory can look like a taxonomy.

We see “Focal mark,” “Binary bearing,” “Boundary act,” and many other vivid labels.

The names are memorable.

They are useful.

But they are not the deepest local objects.

The theoretical order is:

**coordinate → generated form → canonical crystallization → concrete manifestation**

That order matters.

A coordinate specifies structural constraints.

The generated form states their conjunction as an event-shape.

The canonical crystallization selects a stable semantic realization.

The concrete manifestation instantiates that realization in particular material.

This is stronger than saying “the examples belong in boxes.”

It says the boxes themselves have internal generative content.

### Why the 54 positions form one system

Six Transformation Patterns.

Three Completion Topologies.

Three Persistence Modes.

Their Cartesian combination gives fifty-four coordinate positions.

The word “Cartesian” is simply a formal way to say that every value on one axis can be paired with every value on the others in the state-space.

But the important part is not the multiplication alone.

The important part is recurrence of function.

Transformation Pattern always contributes the kind of decisive change.

Completion Topology always contributes the condition of sufficiency.

Persistence Mode always contributes the mode of achieved availability.

The values vary.

The grammatical roles remain.

That is what makes the 54 positions one system.

### The state-space is not just a storage device

A state-space can be used retrospectively.

We can look at an event and ask which coordinate best describes it.

But the grammar also invites a prospective use.

Given a coordinate, we can ask what kind of event should satisfy it.

This is an important change in direction.

Retrospective use:

example → coordinate.

Prospective use:

coordinate → generated form → possible manifestations.

The theory does not yet claim that prospective generation is the same thing as empirical prediction.

It is an internal theoretical operation.

But it is stronger than mere classification.

A taxonomy waits for cases.

A generative grammar constrains the kinds of cases that can belong at a position.

### The same distinction clarifies the role of examples

Examples remain essential.

They make abstract structure recognizable.

They show recurrence across domains.

They allow us to compare generated expectation with experienced cases.

But examples do not create the coordinate.

If all three canonical examples for a cell were replaced, the coordinate-generated form should still be expressible.

That is a useful test of whether the theory is genuinely structural.

The form should survive the loss of any one manifestation.

### Formation history remains a different layer

The generated form also does not tell us the entire history of how the event formed.

A sentence may undergo local completion, reanalysis, repair, and later landing.

A mechanism may be tested, adjusted, fail again, and finally stabilize.

A decision may pass through several provisional choices.

The completed coordinate abstracts from much of that history.

It tells us the achieved structural form.

The historical layer tells us how the event unfolded.

This is another reason “syntax” in phenomenological syntax should not be confused with a temporal recipe.

The coordinate is not a mandatory sequence of stages.

It is a conjunction of completed constraints.

### Intelligibility remains prior to truth

The generative architecture does not change the truth distinction.

A coordinate-generated form can be realized by a false claim.

A complete explanation can be wrong.

A well-formed plan can fail.

A fictional event can be perfectly intelligible.

The grammar describes how something becomes determinate enough for uptake.

Truth and correctness evaluate that determinate result afterward.

Generation therefore concerns completed intelligibility, not guaranteed validity.

### What has actually been demonstrated?

At this level we can state the strongest internal achievement more clearly.

The corpus presents:

- six recurring Transformation Patterns;
- three recurring Completion Topologies;
- three recurring Persistence Modes;
- fifty-four coordinate positions;
- generated event-sentences for those coordinates;
- canonical crystallizations;
- multiple manifestations;
- systematic substitution across the axes.

Together these support the claim that the Atlas is not merely an unordered collection of examples.

It exhibits a compositional architecture.

That is a substantive result.

But we still need a claims boundary.

The architecture does not yet prove:

- that the three dimensions are metaphysically necessary;
- that the 54 positions exhaust all possible completed forms;
- that every actual case has one uniquely correct coordinate;
- that human cognition literally implements the grammar as a mechanism.

Those remain research questions.

### The next distinction appears inside generation itself

Once the coordinate can generate a form before the canonical name is consulted, another question becomes unavoidable.

How tightly does the coordinate determine that form?

Sometimes the generated form and the canonical crystallization nearly coincide.

Sometimes the coordinate leaves more room.

Sometimes a familiar name contributes a substantial semantic sharpening beyond the bare structural constraints.

So the next threshold is not another axis.

It is a distinction between levels of determination:

what the coordinate requires,

what the canonical crystallization adds,

and what a concrete case instantiates.

The grammar has become generative.

Now we need to understand the layers inside what it generates.

## Classification and generation should be tested against one another

A strong grammar should support both directions without contradiction.

Start with the channel gate.

Retrospective route:

the gate makes a relevant difference at one place;
that one place is sufficient;
the result changes what can continue.

So:

Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy.

Now hide the example.

Begin from the coordinate.

Prospective route:

a relevant difference must become operative;
the sufficiency must gather at one privileged locus;
the completed result must remain as changed capacity or consequence.

That yields a focal operative distinction.

Now bring the channel gate back.

It fits.

The two directions meet.

This is a stronger internal test than merely assigning a label after seeing an example.

## Generation can fail, and that is useful

Suppose we try to place a case under Opening + Distributed + Bearing.

We propose a lamp in a room.

The lamp clearly increases availability.

But if one source is sufficient, the topology is Focal rather than Distributed.

The example fails the coordinate.

Or suppose we propose a bridge, but the only relevant remainder is that the bridge itself stands there.

Then Standing may be more appropriate than Bearing.

These failures are useful.

A generative theory should be able to say not only why an example fits, but why a tempting example does not fit.

That gives the grammar diagnostic force.

## Grammar does not mean language-only

The word “grammar” can mislead.

Here it names the systematic combination of recurring roles.

Those roles can be manifested in sentences, but also in routes, repairs, machines, bridges, decisions, and other completed events.

The grammar is therefore not a grammar of English or of sentence syntax.

It is a grammar of completion structure.

The sentence-shaped generated forms are representational tools for expressing the structure.

They do not make linguistic form the ontology.

## One system, many semantic surfaces

This distinction helps explain why the canonical crystallizations are so varied.

Focal mark, Alternate passage, Reciprocal shift, Branching output, Boundary act, and many others do not sound like members of one semantic family.

That is because the semantic surfaces differ.

What unifies them is not vocabulary.

It is coordinate composition.

This is one of the strongest reasons the theory should be read structurally rather than lexically.



## A grammar should explain families, not only individual cells

The generative account becomes stronger when we stop testing one cell at a time.

Take the entire Focal triplet under Differentiation.

Standing gives a distinguished candidate.

Bearing gives a selected candidate whose contrast remains constitutive.

Efficacy gives a focal distinction that changes what can happen.

The three forms are related because they share two constraints.

Now move to Opening while preserving Focal and cycling the same persistence modes.

Standing gives a source field.

Bearing gives source-carried divergence.

Efficacy gives branching output.

The semantic content changes, but the persistence roles recur.

A grammar should make these family resemblances intelligible.

That is what coordinate composition does.

## Why generated sentences matter editorially

The generated sentence forces the writer to expose the contribution of each axis.

If the sentence describes Opening but never makes the topology visible, it is incomplete.

If it describes Focality but leaves the persistence mode ambiguous, it is incomplete.

If it merely repeats the canonical name in longer words, it has not generated anything.

This makes generated sentences a useful discipline for the entire corpus.

They reveal whether the coordinate is actually doing explanatory work.

## The grammar can be wrong in structured ways

A genuine theory should make possible more than successful fits.

Suppose a cell repeatedly requires examples that satisfy only two of the three constraints.

Suppose a canonical name systematically pulls interpretation toward a different topology.

Suppose prospective generation produces a form unlike the published crystallization.

Those would be internal problems.

The grammar therefore exposes itself to correction.

That is stronger than a loose descriptive vocabulary, because the architecture creates specific expectations about what each cell should contain.

## The whole theory in one compact loop

By this stage the reader should be able to run a full loop mentally:

an unresolved field creates pressure;

one kind of transformation answers it;

one topology makes the answer sufficient;

one persistence mode characterizes the achieved availability;

the coordinate generates a structural form;

a canonical crystallization sharpens it;

a manifestation realizes it;

a history explains how it came to govern;

truth and success remain later judgments.

That loop is the whole system in compressed form.

The later essays will not replace it.

They will make each transition more exact.



## The distinction between rule and inventory

An inventory answers: what forms have we observed and named?

A rule answers: what must be true of any form occupying this coordinate?

The Atlas contains both layers.

Its named cells form an inventory of canonical crystallizations.

Its coordinate grammar provides the rule-like structure that explains why those cells belong where they do.

Keeping those layers distinct lets the Atlas be descriptive and generative at once.

That dual character is one of the theory’s central strengths.

## The Structure, the Name, and the Case Are Not the Same Thing

Suppose we know a coordinate:

**Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy**

From the coordinate alone, we can already say something.

A relevant difference must become operative.

The completion must gather around one privileged locus.

The achieved result must remain as a changed capacity, consequence, or continuation.

That gives us a generated form:

**focal operative distinction**.

The Atlas then offers a canonical crystallization:

**Boundary act**.

And we can see concrete manifestations:

a channel gate stops flow;

a finish line ends a run;

a marked point terminates a pencil stroke.

At first, these may seem like four ways of saying the same thing.

They are not.

The coordinate, the generated form, the canonical crystallization, and the manifestation occupy different levels.

Understanding those levels is the next major refinement in the theory.

### The coordinate is the conjunction of primitive values

A coordinate is the triple itself.

It contains one value from each primitive dimension.

For example:

**Differentiation / Focal / Efficacy**

The coordinate is abstract.

It tells us which three structural contributions must be jointly satisfied.

But the three labels by themselves are still compressed.

They do not yet state the event in ordinary language.

### The coordinate-generated form unfolds the structural demand

The **coordinate-generated form** is the local event-shape demanded by the coordinate.

It is more specific than simply listing the three values.

It tells us how those values combine into one completed movement.

For Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy:

a difference becomes concentrated at one locus and becomes operative in what can happen.

That is already a recognizable kind of event.

But it remains broader than the canonical term “Boundary act.”

This is the first important distinction:

**coordinate-generated form is not merely the coordinate labels repeated.**

It is the structural conjunction expressed as phenomenological syntax.

### Structural determination happens before semantic naming

The coordinate does real work before a familiar name is chosen.

It rules out many possibilities.

A form at Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy cannot be primarily about unorganized parts becoming a whole.

That would be Integration.

It cannot become sufficient only through a distributed span if the topology is Focal.

It cannot leave merely an achieved standing if the persistence mode is Efficacy.

The coordinate narrows the admissible structure.

This narrowing is what the mature theory calls **structural determination**.

Structural determination answers:

**How much does the coordinate itself require before we choose a particular semantic realization?**

That question will become even more important when we compare tightly constrained and more open coordinates.

### Canonical crystallization adds semantic specificity

The **canonical crystallization** is a selected stable realization of the generated form.

“Boundary act” is one such crystallization.

It is excellent because a boundary makes focal operative differentiation easy to recognize.

But “boundary” is already more semantically specific than the bare generated form.

The coordinate requires an operative distinction at one locus.

The canonical name chooses boundary-like termination as the stable semantic realization.

That choice is not arbitrary.

It is strongly guided by the coordinate.

But it is still a choice at a later theoretical level.

This is why the term **crystallization** is useful.

Something structurally constrained becomes semantically sharper.

### Semantic individuation is different from structural determination

The mature theory calls this further sharpening **semantic individuation**.

Structural determination says what the coordinate requires.

Semantic individuation says which more specific recognizable phenomenon is selected inside that allowed region.

This is another distinction that prevents overclaiming.

If we confuse semantic individuation with structural determination, we may start treating every feature of the canonical name as though it were forced by the coordinate.

That would make the theory say more than its own architecture warrants.

### Concrete manifestation comes later still

A **concrete manifestation** is an actual case.

The channel gate is a manifestation.

The finish line is another.

The pencil endpoint is another.

Manifestations contain details that neither the coordinate nor the canonical crystallization requires.

The channel has water.

The finish line has runners.

The pencil example has a drawn mark.

Those details belong to the material.

They help us see the form.

But they are not part of the definition.

This gives us a clear hierarchy:

**coordinate**

→ **coordinate-generated form**

→ **canonical crystallization**

→ **concrete manifestation**

Each step adds specificity.

### Why this hierarchy matters for the 54-position Atlas

Without this distinction, the Atlas can easily be misunderstood.

It may look like each coordinate is simply identical with its canonical name.

But that is not always true.

Some coordinates are so constraining that the generated form and crystallization nearly coincide.

Others leave a wider semantic region.

The Atlas therefore has two local levels:

- what the coordinate itself generates;
- the canonical realization selected to make that region memorable and usable.

This allows the theory to remain systematic without pretending that every canonical term is logically forced in every detail.

### Cell 1 shows a very narrow case

Consider:

**Differentiation + Focal + Standing**

Generated form:

**focal distinguished standing**

Canonical crystallization:

**Focal mark**

Manifestations:

one key pointed out;

one star singled out;

one face indicated.

Here the coordinate is already extremely narrow.

A relevant difference is concentrated on one candidate.

The result simply stands as the distinguished one.

There is little semantic room left.

The crystallization “Focal mark” adds relatively little beyond making the event memorable.

This is why the Atlas treats this cell as close to structurally determined.

### Cell 3 shows a wider case

Now return to:

**Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy**

Generated form:

**focal operative distinction**

Canonical crystallization:

**Boundary act**

The coordinate clearly requires a focal distinction with operative consequence.

But “boundary” is one way of realizing that.

Other focal operative distinctions may be possible in principle.

The canonical term therefore contributes more semantic individuation than in Cell 1.

Both coordinates are structurally meaningful.

They simply differ in how much semantic latitude remains after the coordinate constraints are satisfied.

We are not yet naming the full scale of constraint strength.

But the difference is now visible.

### The same hierarchy can be seen in Opening

Take:

**Opening + Distributed + Bearing**

**span-bearing reach**

**Span-carried reach**

Manifestations:

a bridge making the far bank reachable;

Here the generated form and crystallization are close.

The structural shape remains stable.

### The same hierarchy protects cross-domain comparison

Suppose we compare a bridge and a network cable.

If we compare them as objects, they are quite different.

If we compare them as manifestations of span-carried reach, their common structure becomes visible.

This is one of the main uses of the hierarchy.

The generated form gives the coordinate-required structure.

The coordinate gives the primitive conjunction.

Each level supports a different kind of comparison.

### Formation history is another level that must remain separate

The hierarchy above concerns local completed form and representation.

Formation history is different again.

A bridge may be built in stages.

A network may be rerouted after failed attempts.

A sentence may undergo provisional parsing and later reanalysis.

The same completed coordinate can emerge through different histories.

So we now have at least two distinct kinds of layering:

**completed-form layering**

coordinate → generated form → crystallization → manifestation

and

**historical layering**

opening → local operations → governing transformation → landing → persistent result

We are not yet using the full mature historical vocabulary everywhere.

But the distinction matters.

A theory can describe both what form was achieved and how that form came to govern the situation.

Those are related explanatory tasks, not identical ones.

### Intelligibility before truth also becomes a level distinction

A false diagnosis can instantiate a completed form.

A fictional event can instantiate a completed form.

A bad decision can instantiate a completed form.

This means truth and correctness belong to another evaluative level.

The grammar describes the organization that makes a result determinate enough for uptake.

Epistemic or practical evaluation asks whether that result deserves endorsement.

Keeping these levels separate avoids a different kind of overclaim.

The theory is not saying that whatever completes is true.

It is saying that something generally needs sufficient determinacy before truth or falsity can even be assessed.

### Why the language of “form” needs care

At this stage the word **form** can become dangerous because it may refer to several levels.

We can mean:

- completed form in general;
- coordinate-generated form;
- canonical crystallization;
- concrete manifestation.

The mature glossary therefore recommends being specific when ambiguity matters.

That is not pedantry.

It is a way of keeping the architecture intact.

If we say “the form is Boundary act,” do we mean the generated coordinate-region?

The selected crystallization?

A particular boundary manifestation?

Those are different claims.

Precision about vocabulary protects precision about theory.

### The 54 positions are regions of structured possibility

We can now improve our picture of the state-space.

Each coordinate is not merely a point with a name attached.

It specifies a local region of admissible completed form.

For some coordinates, the region is very narrow.

For others, it is wider.

The canonical crystallization selects a stable realization inside that region.

Concrete manifestations occupy particular places within the realization.

This gives the Atlas depth.

The 54 positions are not only 54 labels.

They are 54 conjunctions of constraints with varying degrees of semantic freedom.

### What the theory can and cannot claim at this level

The hierarchy strengthens the theory internally.

It explains why canonical examples can vary while the generated form remains stable.

It explains why a canonical name can be informative without being identical to the coordinate.

It explains why some coordinates seem nearly forced while others are semantically more open.

But again, internal clarification is not the same as external proof.

The theory still does not establish:

- that every coordinate has one objectively best crystallization;
- that every manifestation will be classified identically by independent observers;
- that the 54 regions exhaust all possible completed intelligibility;
- that the dimensions are implemented as separate cognitive mechanisms.

The architecture tells us what to test.

It does not allow us to skip the testing.

### The next question is now unavoidable

We have separated:

the coordinate,

the generated form,

the canonical crystallization,

and the concrete manifestation.

We can also see that some coordinates leave almost no semantic room, while others leave more.

So the next question becomes precise:

**How strongly does a coordinate constrain its admissible realizations?**

That question requires us to move from a simple yes-or-no idea of determination to a graded one.

Some generated regions are narrow.

Some are broader.

The grammar constrains all of them.

But it does not constrain all of them equally.

## Why the hierarchy prevents category mistakes

The four levels answer different questions.

The coordinate asks:

which primitive values are combined?

The generated form asks:

what event-shape follows from those values?

The canonical crystallization asks:

what stable semantic realization will represent that region?

The concrete manifestation asks:

where does such a realization actually appear?

Confusing these questions produces predictable mistakes.

If manifestation is confused with coordinate, examples become definitions.

If crystallization is confused with generated form, the canonical name looks logically necessary.

If generated form is confused with coordinate, the compositional roles become hidden.

Keeping the levels separate lets each do its own work.

## A canonical crystallization can be excellent without being inevitable

This is an important subtlety.

A canonical crystallization can be theoretically excellent.

It can make the generated region immediately recognizable.

It can compress a complex event-shape into a memorable phrase.

It can support reliable exposition.

None of that requires saying it is the only possible crystallization.

The repository’s distinction allows canonicity without metaphysical necessity.

That is a useful kind of theoretical discipline.

## Concrete manifestations contain excess detail

Every manifestation contains more information than the grammar needs.

A bridge has materials, dimensions, ownership, location, weather conditions, history, cost, and design.

Most of that is irrelevant to its manifestation of span-carried reach.

A photograph contains lighting, clothing, background, camera angle, and many other details.

Most of that is irrelevant to focal distinguished standing.

The grammar therefore performs selective abstraction.

It does not deny the excess detail.

It simply identifies which part of the event is structurally relevant to the coordinate.

## Representation becomes layered rather than flat

At earlier levels, we could say “shape, name, example.”

Now the theory can support a layered representation:

primitive coordinate values constrain a local event-shape;
the event-shape defines a region;
the canonical crystallization selects a stable semantic form within that region;
the manifestation instantiates it in material.

That layering is one of the points where the theory becomes recognizably academic rather than merely intuitive.



## Different levels support different kinds of disagreement

The hierarchy also helps us diagnose disagreement.

Two analysts may agree on the coordinate but prefer different canonical descriptions.

That is a disagreement about semantic individuation.

Two analysts may agree on the crystallization but disagree about whether a particular case manifests it.

That is a disagreement about application.

Two analysts may disagree about the coordinate itself.

That is a deeper disagreement about structural analysis.

Without the hierarchy, all three disputes can look like “classification disagreement.”

With the hierarchy, we can ask exactly where the divergence occurs.

This matters later for empirical reliability.

## The hierarchy also preserves portability

A good generated form should travel across domains.

A canonical crystallization can remain stable enough to organize exposition.

A manifestation can change freely in material.

This combination gives the theory both stability and portability.

Too much abstraction would make the generated form empty.

Too much semantic specificity would trap the theory inside one domain.

The layered architecture allows structural invariance and material diversity at once.

## Why canonical crystallization is not merely a nickname

The canonical term is not decorative.

It can encode a particularly revealing semantic realization.

“Span-carried reach” does more than shorten a generated sentence.

It identifies a compact phenomenon in which the coordinate contributions become easy to recognize together.

“Reciprocal shift” highlights mutual alteration rather than generic relation.

“Boundary act” highlights operative termination rather than generic difference.

The crystallization therefore has theoretical value.

Its status is simply downstream from the coordinate-generated form.

## Why manifestation is indispensable despite being downstream

Concrete cases keep the theory answerable to recognizable experience.

A generated form that cannot be manifested intelligibly would be a reason to re-examine the analysis.

A canonical crystallization that supports only one strained example would be a reason to re-examine whether it represents the generated region well.

Manifestations therefore test whether abstraction has remained connected to phenomena.

The hierarchy is not a ladder from unimportant to important.

It is a division of theoretical labor.



## The hierarchy gives each kind of evidence a proper place

Different evidence bears on different levels.

A cross-domain example can support the recognizability of a manifestation pattern.

Agreement on a canonical crystallization can support semantic stability.

Successful blind generation can support the structural determination supplied by a coordinate.

Historical reconstruction can support the distinction between completed form and formation.

No single kind of evidence validates every level at once.

The hierarchy therefore helps organize not only the theory but its future evidence.

## Layered errors require layered repairs

If a manifestation is weak, replace or reanalyze the example.

If a canonical crystallization is misleading, revise the semantic label.

If the generated form fails to express one of the primitive contributions, revise the generated sentence.

If repeated problems arise at the coordinate level, the primitive architecture itself may need revision.

This gives the theory a useful repair structure.

It need not treat every local problem as a crisis of the entire system.

## How Much Does a Coordinate Decide?

We have reached a point where the coordinate can do real theoretical work.

Given a Transformation Pattern, a Completion Topology, and a Persistence Mode, we can generate a local event-shape before consulting the familiar name or example.

But generation raises a new question.

Does every coordinate determine its realization equally tightly?

No.

Some coordinate triples leave almost no room beyond wording or material.

Others define a clear structural region while leaving more semantic latitude.

This does not weaken the grammar.

It tells us something more exact about how constraint works.

### Structural determination can be strong without being absolute

Take:

**Differentiation + Focal + Standing**

The coordinate requires a relevant difference concentrated on one candidate, with that candidate remaining as the achieved result.

It is difficult to move far from the event of indication.

One key is singled out.

One star is identified.

One face is indicated.

The canonical crystallization **Focal mark** adds very little semantic structure beyond what the coordinate already demands.

The coordinate is therefore close to determining its crystallization.

Now compare:

**Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy**

The coordinate requires a focal difference that changes what can happen.

The canonical crystallization **Boundary act** is a strong realization.

A gate stops flow.

A finish line ends a run.

A marked point terminates a line.

But “boundary” is not the only imaginable semantic realization of a focal operative distinction.

The coordinate is still structurally clear.

It simply leaves more room.

The mature theory calls this difference **constraint strength**.

### Three useful grades of constraint strength

The Atlas uses three broad judgments.

**Near-determinate**

The coordinate leaves very little beyond wording, domain, or minor semantic detail.

**Tightly constrained**

The coordinate defines a narrow local family, but the canonical crystallization still adds meaningful semantic specificity.

**Open realization**

The coordinate fixes a recognizable phenomenological syntax while allowing a broader family of semantic realizations.

These are not probabilities.

“Open realization” does not mean the coordinate is vague.

“Near-determinate” does not mean the crystallization is metaphysically necessary.

The labels describe how much semantic latitude remains after the structural constraints are jointly satisfied.

### The admissible region

This gives us another useful concept.

A coordinate defines an **admissible region**.

That region contains the kinds of completed forms that satisfy all three constraints.

The region may be narrow.

It may be broader.

But it is not limitless.

For Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy, an admissible realization must still involve:

- Differentiation;
- focal sufficiency;
- operative consequence.

A story about several parts gaining roles through a whole would not belong there.

A result that merely stands without any operative consequence would not belong there.

The coordinate therefore excludes as well as permits.

This is why semantic freedom should not be confused with arbitrariness.

### Semantic freedom is bounded

The mature theory uses **semantic freedom** for the latitude that remains after the coordinate constraints have been satisfied.

Freedom here means room for more than one semantic realization.

It does not mean “anything goes.”

A coordinate can be structurally determinate and semantically open at the same time.

That distinction is important because it avoids two opposite errors.

The first error is overconstraint:

treating every feature of the canonical crystallization as forced by the coordinate.

The second is underconstraint:

treating the coordinate as so abstract that it imposes almost no real structure.

The theory takes the middle path.

The coordinate determines a region.

The crystallization selects a stable semantic realization inside that region.

The manifestation realizes that crystallization in material.

### Opening pressure also constrains without fully determining

The same logic appears earlier in the event.

Opening pressure constrains the kind of answer that could count.

A blocked route requires some form of increased availability.

But it does not determine in advance whether that availability will come from a focal source, a distributed span, or release from a restriction.

So constraint already appears before the coordinate is complete.

The unresolved field narrows the relevant transformation.

The topology narrows how sufficiency can be achieved.

The persistence mode narrows what kind of completed availability remains.

The coordinate-generated form is the product of those constraints.

### The six transformations remain stable under this richer account

Constraint strength does not change the six Transformation Patterns.

Differentiation still makes a difference operative.

Opening still increases availability.

Relating still establishes constitutive bearing.

Repair still displaces governed misfit.

Integration still gives parts roles through a whole.

Settlement still discharges residual demand.

What changes from coordinate to coordinate is how tightly the full conjunction determines a semantic region.

This is important.

The theory is not saying that some Transformation Patterns are vague and others precise.

Constraint strength belongs to the coordinate as a whole.

### Topology can narrow the region dramatically

Compare two Opening coordinates.

**Opening + Focal + Standing**

A field becomes available from one privileged source.

The local form narrows quickly.

The canonical crystallization **Source field** is strongly guided.

Now consider:

**Opening + Release + Bearing**

The coordinate requires:

- increased availability;
- achieved through release from prior governance;
- with the result remaining through a constitutive bearing.

That structure is recognizable.

But its semantic realization may admit more than one local form.

The topology and persistence mode together can therefore change the width of the region significantly.

### Persistence Mode can also alter semantic freedom

Standing often leaves the achieved determination itself available.

That can make some coordinates semantically narrow.

Bearing can introduce a range of possible constitutive relations.

Efficacy can introduce a range of possible consequences or capacities.

But this is not a universal ranking.

We should not assume Standing is always narrow and Efficacy always broad.

Constraint strength is a property of the full coordinate conjunction.

The grammar has to be read locally.

### Why the canonical crystallization remains necessary

If the generated form is fundamental, why bother with canonical crystallizations?

Because a theory needs stable handles.

The generated form can be abstract.

A canonical crystallization makes a region memorable and operationally usable.

“Boundary act” is easier to work with than repeating the full generated sentence every time.

“Span-carried reach” makes a structural region recognizable at a glance.

“Reciprocal shift” gives a stable reference for a distributed relational bearing.

The crystallization therefore plays a representational role.

It sharpens and stabilizes without becoming identical to the structural region.

### Manifestations test recognizability, not necessity

Concrete manifestations are equally important.

If a generated form is supposed to be domain-general, it should be recognizable in more than one material.

A strong cell therefore benefits from examples that differ substantially in subject matter while preserving the same event-shape.

But even several good examples do not prove the coordinate is universal.

They show that the form can recur.

They help establish phenomenological recognizability.

They do not establish metaphysical necessity.

### Formation history can cross the same admissible region in different ways

Suppose two events end in the same generated form.

One arrives directly.

Another arrives through correction and reanalysis.

A third arrives through several local completions.

The completed form may lie in the same admissible region even though the histories differ.

This reinforces the distinction between completed form and historical formation.

Constraint strength concerns the width of the completed-form region.

It does not tell us how many historical paths can lead there.

A narrow coordinate could still have many formation histories.

A broad coordinate could be reached by a very simple history.

These are different dimensions of description.

### Truth is still orthogonal to constraint strength

A near-determinate completed form can be false.

An open realization can be true.

Constraint strength does not measure epistemic quality.

It measures structural-semantic determination within the grammar.

This is another reason the theory separates intelligibility from truth.

A result can be very tightly structured and still mistaken.

A result can admit semantic latitude and still be correct.

The axes, constraint strength, and truth evaluation answer different questions.

### The 54-position Atlas becomes a map of regions

The state-space can now be pictured differently.

Earlier, it looked like fifty-four positions.

Now each position can be treated as a structured local region with a characteristic width.

The coordinate gives the structural centre of the region.

The generated form makes that structure explicit.

The canonical crystallization selects a stable semantic realization.

The manifestations show concrete cases.

This makes the Atlas more than a grid.

It becomes a map of constrained possibilities.

### What this adds to the claims boundary

The distinction between constraint strength and necessity is especially important for research claims.

If the theory labels a coordinate Near-determinate, that is an internal structural judgment.

It does not by itself show that independent observers will classify cases with high agreement.

If a coordinate is an Open realization, that does not predict confusion or low reliability.

Those are empirical questions.

Likewise, the existence of bounded admissible regions does not prove that the whole 54-position system is exhaustive.

The theory can be internally strong while remaining empirically open.

### The next question

We now understand completed forms more precisely.

We can distinguish their coordinate, generated region, canonical crystallization, manifestations, and degree of constraint.

But we have mostly been looking at the completed result.

Events also have histories.

They open.

They develop locally.

They can be revised.

One operation may eventually govern the whole.

They land.

A result remains.

The next step is to distinguish the architecture of completed form from the history through which completion was achieved.

## Three neighboring cells show three different widths

The constraint-strength idea becomes clearer when several nearby cells are compared rather than described in isolation.

Start with Differentiation + Focal + Standing.

Almost everything important is already forced. A relevant difference is concentrated on one candidate, and that candidate remains as the distinguished one. “Focal mark” is therefore very close to the generated form.

Now the distinction must become operative. “Boundary act” is a lucid crystallization, but the region is broader because efficacy can be expressed through several kinds of consequential difference.

The theory’s explanatory strategy should change depending on the cell.

Later essays must reveal where the handle has been doing more semantic work than the coordinate itself.

That revelation should sharpen the earlier account, not discredit it.

Once a coordinate defines an admissible region, examples can be assessed more rigorously.

This does not yet produce a mechanical classifier.

But it provides explicit reasons for accepting or rejecting a proposed placement.

That is an important step toward later empirical work.

The width of a generated region depends on how much semantic structure is jointly forced by the three primitive contributions.

Some conjunctions converge sharply.

Differentiation + Focal + Standing almost forces an event of indication or selection.

Other conjunctions leave more ways to realize the same functional roles.

Efficacy often permits different kinds of operative consequence.

Bearing can be realized through contrast, dependence, support, source, condition, or reciprocity.

Release can apply to different kinds of prior governance.

The width therefore emerges from the conjunction.

It should not be assigned by intuition alone.

## Constraint-strength judgments should themselves be revisable

Near-determinate, Tightly constrained, and Open realization are part of the current theoretical description.

They are not immune from revision.

A coordinate initially treated as open may prove narrower after more examples.

A supposedly near-determinate cell may admit legitimate alternative crystallizations.

Independent analysts may disagree systematically about width.

This makes constraint strength another empirical and editorial research target.

The labels are useful because they expose the question rather than hiding it.

## Semantic freedom is necessary for cross-domain use

If every coordinate forced one narrow semantic content, the grammar would have difficulty transferring across domains.

A bridge, a cable, and a social connection can share a structural pattern only if the generated region abstracts away from domain-specific material.

Semantic freedom is therefore not merely tolerated.

Some degree of it is required for domain-general structure.

The challenge is to preserve enough freedom for transfer while keeping enough constraint for discrimination.

That balance is central to the grammar.

## The whole Atlas can be read as a distribution of widths

Once constraint strength is explicit, the 54-position state-space gains another descriptive layer.

Each coordinate has:
- a location in the 6 × 3 × 3 architecture;
- a generated event-shape;
- a semantic width;
- a canonical crystallization;
- a manifestation set.

This richer representation will matter if the theory is ever operationalized computationally or empirically.

A classifier may need not only a coordinate prediction but a confidence or admissibility account for neighboring realizations.

The present theory does not yet supply a metric.

But it has identified the dimension along which such formalization would have to occur.



## Width is not vagueness

An Open realization can still have crisp boundaries.

A region may admit several semantic realizations while excluding many others.

This is familiar in ordinary concepts.

A “tool” admits many material forms without including every object.

Likewise, a generated region can permit several crystallizations while preserving structural identity.

The important question is whether the boundaries of admissibility can be stated clearly.

If they cannot, semantic freedom has become vagueness.

If they can, openness is a controlled property of the coordinate.

## Constraint strength should affect confidence about naming, not confidence about the coordinate itself

A coordinate may be confidently identified while its best crystallization remains debatable.

This is another reason to distinguish levels.

Analysts might agree strongly that a case is Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy while disagreeing about whether “Boundary act” is the most revealing canonical term.

That would be a semantic disagreement, not necessarily a structural one.

Future reliability studies should preserve this distinction rather than forcing every disagreement into one score.

## The Finished Form and the History That Produced It

A completed event can look simple after it is over.

A sentence has been understood.

A machine has been repaired.

A route has been found.

A decision has been settled.

But the path to that completed result may have been complicated.

There may have been provisional interpretations, local completions, failed attempts, corrections, competing pressures, and a final reorganization that made one structure govern the whole.

The theory therefore needs two descriptive layers.

One describes **what completed form was achieved**.

The other describes **how that form came to be achieved**.

These layers should not be collapsed.

### The coordinate describes completed organization

The 6 × 3 × 3 grammar gives us coordinates of completed form.

A coordinate tells us:

- what kind of unresolvedness changed;
- what topology made that change sufficient;
- how the achieved result remains available.

The coordinate-generated form expresses those constraints as a local event-syntax.

The canonical crystallization sharpens the semantic realization.

The manifestation gives us a concrete case.

None of these, by itself, tells us every historical step.

The coordinate is therefore not a timeline.

It is a structural description of achieved completion.

### Historical formation begins with an opening

Before completion, there is an unresolved field.

The mature theory calls the structured incompletion **opening pressure**.

When we describe the history of an event, we can also speak of its **opening**: the initial phase in which the unresolved organization becomes active and begins to guide what further uptake is owed.

This requires care.

**Opening** with a capital O is one of the six Transformation Patterns.

It means increased availability.

**opening** as a historical phase is different.

Any Transformation Pattern can have a historical opening.

A Repair event can open.

A Settlement event can open.

A Differentiation event can open.

The same word appears at two theoretical levels, so capitalization and context matter.

### Local operations can complete before the whole does

Consider a sentence made of several clauses.

One clause may already be complete enough to understand locally.

Another clause may also be locally complete.

But the whole sentence may still owe us a relation between them.

A conditional can reorganize two locally intelligible clauses into a larger dependency.

An attribution can wrap an already understandable quoted statement inside a larger act.

A later phrase can force us to revise how an earlier phrase was organized.

This is why **local completion** and **global completion** are distinct.

A part can finish its local work while remaining open to reorganization by a larger whole.

The Lego tower inside the unfinished castle was an early version of this idea.

Now we can state it more exactly.

### Governing transformation

Many operations can occur during an event.

Not all of them define the completed whole.

A sentence may contain a local distinction, a local relation, and a local repair.

A mechanism may undergo several adjustments.

A decision may involve comparisons, exclusions, and revisions.

The mature historical account therefore asks which transformation ultimately **governs** the completed organization.

The **governing transformation** is not simply the last event in time.

It is the change whose organization determines the final completed whole.

This concept is especially important in sentences because later material can reveal which earlier operations were subordinate.

### Landing

At some point, the governing organization becomes sufficiently determinate.

The theory calls this **landing**.

Landing is not necessarily the final word, final second, or final physical motion.

It is the point or span at which the event’s governing organization becomes settled enough that the completed form can be recovered.

A sentence may land before its final punctuation.

A decision may land before it is announced.

A repair may land when the governing fault has been displaced, even though testing continues.

Landing is therefore historical.

It describes when the completed organization becomes determinate.

It is not one of the three primitive coordinate axes.

### Persistent result

After landing, an achieved result remains available.

This is where historical formation reconnects with Persistence Mode.

The historical account can speak of a **persistent result**.

The coordinate account specifies whether that result is available primarily as Standing, Bearing, or Efficacy.

The two layers meet without becoming identical.

The history tells us how the result formed.

The coordinate tells us what kind of completed availability was achieved.

### A garden-path sentence shows why history matters

Sentence processing gave the theory an especially vivid observational case.

A garden-path sentence leads the reader toward one provisional interpretation.

Later material makes that interpretation fail.

Earlier material has to be reorganized.

The final sentence can become fully intelligible.

But the final form alone does not show the history of provisional commitment and repair.

That history matters if we want to understand how completion was achieved.

Yet the history is not itself the coordinate.

This is one of the clearest reasons the mature theory separates completed form from formation.

### The same coordinate can have different histories

Imagine two mechanisms that end in the same Repair coordinate.

One is corrected immediately because the fault is obvious.

The other is tested repeatedly, partly adjusted, misdiagnosed, and finally repaired.

Their completed form may be structurally comparable.

Their historical trajectories differ.

Likewise, two decisions can end in the same Settlement form.

One may be immediate.

The other may involve prolonged comparison and re-evaluation.

The coordinate abstracts from the trajectory.

This is a strength if used correctly.

It lets the theory compare completed forms across very different histories.

But it would be a mistake to infer the history from the coordinate alone.

### Different coordinates can also emerge from similar histories

The reverse can happen.

Two events may have similar-looking histories of trial, revision, and landing.

Yet their completed forms differ.

One may ultimately be Repair.

Another may be Differentiation.

Another may be Settlement.

Historical similarity does not guarantee coordinate identity.

Again, the two descriptive layers answer different questions.

### Why sentences were such a productive starting point

Sentences unfold in time.

That makes local and global organization unusually visible.

A reader can feel unresolvedness.

A phrase can seem complete.

Later material can reopen or reorganize it.

A sentence can land.

The resulting meaning can persist for further discourse.

This made sentence resolution a privileged observational window into completed intelligibility.

But the mature theory does not therefore treat sentence processing as the ontology of the system.

The same distinction between formation history and completed form can appear in machines, decisions, routes, organizations, and other domains.

Sentences revealed the architecture.

They do not define its full scope.

### The 54-position grammar remains a completed-form state-space

This distinction helps us understand the Atlas more accurately.

The 54 positions are not 54 histories.

They are 54 coordinate positions of completed form.

Each coordinate specifies:

Transformation Pattern × Completion Topology × Persistence Mode.

Historical formation can be much richer.

The same coordinate may be reached through different sequences.

Local operations may come and go.

Reanalysis may occur.

Different moments may carry temporary importance.

The Atlas compresses that history into the achieved structural result.

### Generated form and history should not be confused

A coordinate-generated sentence often sounds eventive.

It may say that one condition changes until another result remains.

That does not mean it is a literal chronological script.

Its event-shape expresses functional roles.

The historical event may instantiate those roles through a complex path.

So phenomenological syntax is not a recipe.

It is the abstract structure of completed movement.

Historical formation is the actual unfolding.

### Truth and historical confidence are separate again

An interpretation may land confidently and later prove wrong.

A diagnosis may become stable enough to guide action and later be revised.

A sentence may receive a provisional but determinate reading that is later corrected by larger context.

This shows again that landing and truth are not identical.

Landing means the governing organization has become determinate enough for uptake at that stage.

Truth or correctness evaluates the achieved content by another standard.

A later reopening does not prove there was no earlier completion.

It shows that completed forms can enter larger histories.

### Completion is nested across time

This gives us a richer version of the early Lego-castle intuition.

Completions can be nested.

A local unit completes.

A larger unit reorganizes it.

The larger unit completes.

That result enters a still larger discourse, plan, system, or history.

Completion is therefore indexed to a governing demand.

It is not absolute closure.

This is one of the most important protections against misunderstanding the word “completed.”

### What kind of theory does this produce?

The framework is now clearly both structural and process-sensitive.

It is structural because it distinguishes completed forms by recurring functional contributions.

It is process-sensitive because those forms arise through histories of opening, local organization, governing transformation, landing, and persistence.

But it is not a general theory that everything in reality is process.

Its target remains completed intelligibility.

It asks how structured incompletion becomes sufficiently organized for determinate uptake.

Historical formation gives one explanatory layer.

Coordinate grammar gives another.

### Claims boundary

The historical layer creates new research questions.

Can independent observers identify landing points reliably?

Can they distinguish local from governing transformations?

Do different histories converge on the same coordinates in reproducible ways?

Does the history add predictive value beyond the completed-form classification?

Those questions are not settled merely because the concepts are coherent.

The corpus demonstrates a useful distinction between completed form and historical formation.

External validation of that distinction remains open.

### The next structural view

Once the Atlas is kept separate from historical sequence, its own internal organization becomes easier to see.

The 54 coordinates are not ordered by the chronology of events.

They are ordered by recurring structural roles.

Six Transformation families.

Within each, three topologies.

Within each topology, three persistence modes.

This creates a nested repetition.

The next step is to read that repetition not as visual table design, but as a genuine periodic architecture.

## Retrospective stabilization

Historical formation becomes especially interesting when later material changes the status of earlier material.

A sentence may begin with an interpretation that is locally reasonable.

Later words can show that the earlier reading was incomplete or misassigned.

The final organization can then retrospectively stabilize the roles of the earlier parts.

This does not mean the past literally changes.

It means the completed whole determines how earlier elements are finally taken up.

The same pattern can occur outside language.

A decision that initially looks arbitrary may become intelligible after a later constraint is revealed.

A repair step that seemed incidental may turn out to have been the decisive correction once the mechanism is understood.

A route choice may only become intelligible after the destination is known.

Historical formation therefore has a retrospective dimension.

The completed whole can govern the final interpretation of its own formation history.

## Local completion is real even when later reorganization occurs

If later material can reorganize an earlier part, we might be tempted to say the earlier part was never complete.

That would be too strong.

A clause can be locally complete enough for provisional uptake.

A tower can be complete as a tower before becoming part of a castle.

A sub-decision can be settled before a larger decision reorganizes its significance.

Local completion is therefore genuine but indexed.

It is complete relative to a local demand.

Later completion can place it inside a larger organization.

This preserves the theory’s lossless account of nested completion.

## Governing transformation is not simply the largest event

The governing transformation should also be distinguished from scale.

A small local change can govern a much larger completed organization.

A single qualifying phrase can reorganize an entire sentence.

A single corrected connection can make an entire mechanism function.

A final distinction can settle a complex dispute.

What makes a transformation governing is not physical size or duration.

It is configurational control over the completed whole.

This becomes one of the historical questions:

which operation determines the roles of the other operations in the final organization?

## History can be underdetermined by the result

The mature theory should resist reconstructing more history than the completed evidence warrants.

Several histories may converge on one completed form.

Historical reconstruction therefore carries its own uncertainty.

That is another reason to separate classification of completed form from claims about process.



## Formation history can itself be nested

A large event may contain several smaller histories.

A sentence can contain a clause that opens, develops, and locally lands before the larger sentence does.

A repair can contain diagnostic subproblems that each settle before the governing repair is achieved.

A decision can contain local comparisons and exclusions before the final choice lands.

This nesting means there is no single absolute scale of formation.

The analyst must specify which governing demand defines the event under description.

That is another reason completion is indexed rather than absolute.

## Reopening does not erase prior completion

A completed result can later become unresolved again.

A settled policy can be reconsidered.

A repaired mechanism can develop a new fault.

A sentence interpretation can be reopened by later discourse.

This does not show that the earlier completion was unreal.

It shows that completed results can enter new opening fields.

The theory therefore permits cycles of completion and reopening without turning completion into permanent closure.

## Historical description can be richer than coordinate classification

A coordinate may be compact enough to support comparison across domains.

Historical formation may retain details that matter for explanation but disappear from the coordinate.

Which local operation failed first?

Which interpretation was provisional?

When did a later element acquire configurational control?

How many local completions occurred before landing?

These questions belong to the history.

The theory gains explanatory range by allowing both compact state-space classification and richer process reconstruction.

## The sentence case reveals uptake control

One reason sentence histories are revealing is that completion is not merely syntactic ending.

The relevant question is when the sentence becomes stable enough for the next uptake.

A final attribution may change who is responsible for a statement without changing the quoted content.

A conditional may reorganize how two propositions bear on one another.

A later phrase may determine which earlier interpretation should govern.

The history therefore concerns control over uptake, not simply temporal order.

This helps connect historical formation back to completed intelligibility.



## Formation history introduces questions of scope

Every historical analysis must specify what event is being treated as the whole.

In a conversation, the whole might be one sentence, one exchange, or the entire discussion.

In a repair, it might be one adjustment, the complete diagnostic episode, or the restoration of the larger system.

Different scope choices can produce different legitimate local histories.

The theory therefore needs disciplined scope selection.

Otherwise analysts may disagree because they are classifying different wholes rather than because they understand the same whole differently.

## Counterfactual centrality can help identify the governing transformation

One useful historical question is:

if this operation had not occurred, would the completed whole still have had the same organization?

A local detail may be temporally late but structurally peripheral.

Another operation may occur earlier yet be indispensable to the final form.

This counterfactual test can help distinguish local operation from governing transformation.

It is not infallible, but it makes the historical analysis more explicit.

## Landing can be a span rather than an instant

Some events do not become determinate at one exact point.

A decision may stabilize over several exchanges.

A sentence may become effectively settled across a closing phrase.

A repair may become trustworthy only through a short testing sequence.

Landing can therefore be treated as a point or span of determination.

That flexibility is more faithful to formation than forcing every event into a single instant.



## The completed coordinate remains present while we study its history

Historical analysis does not replace the three questions.

Even when the essay is concentrating on formation, the completed event can still be described by asking what Transformation Pattern governed the change, whether sufficiency was Focal, Distributed, or Release, and whether the achieved result persisted as Standing, Bearing, or Efficacy.

The historical layer adds a different question:

**How did this particular completed organization become the one that governed uptake?**

That is why formation can deepen the grammar without becoming a fourth axis.

## The Atlas Repeats Roles, Not Meanings

The 54-position Atlas has a visible pattern.

There are six large families.

Each family contains nine positions.

Inside each nine-position family, the three Completion Topologies recur.

Inside each topology, Standing, Bearing, and Efficacy recur.

At first this can look like table design.

But the mature theory claims something stronger.

The repetition occurs in the functional roles that generate the forms.

The Atlas is **periodic**.

### The slow rhythm: Transformation Pattern

The six Transformation Patterns organize the largest blocks.

Positions 1–9 are Differentiation.

Positions 10–18 are Opening.

Then come Relating, Repair, Integration, and Settlement in their own nine-position families.

Each Transformation Pattern therefore supplies a slow rhythm across nine coordinates.

Within the Differentiation block, every coordinate answers some version of:

how does a previously unavailable or non-operative difference become determinate enough to matter?

Within the Opening block:

how does availability increase relative to prior restriction?

Within Relating:

how does a constitutive bearing become established?

The transformation changes every nine positions.

The other two axes cycle inside it.

### The middle rhythm: Completion Topology

Within each nine-position block, the three topologies occur in groups of three.

First Focal.

Then Distributed.

Then Release.

So within Differentiation:

- Focal / Standing
- Focal / Bearing
- Focal / Efficacy

then:

- Distributed / Standing
- Distributed / Bearing
- Distributed / Efficacy

then:

- Release / Standing
- Release / Bearing
- Release / Efficacy

The same topology rhythm repeats under Opening.

Then under Relating.

Then under Repair, Integration, and Settlement.

This means Focal does not belong to one Transformation Pattern.

Distributed does not belong to one.

Release does not belong to one.

Each recurs as a structural role across the full system.

### The fast rhythm: Persistence Mode

Within every topology triplet, the Persistence Modes repeat:

Standing.

Bearing.

Efficacy.

Then Standing.

Bearing.

Efficacy again.

This is the fastest rhythm in the Atlas.

The transformation may stay fixed for nine positions.

The topology may stay fixed for three.

Persistence changes at each step.

This nested repetition gives the state-space its periodic structure.

### Read one nine-cell family as a conjugation

Consider Differentiation.

**Focal + Standing**

One candidate becomes the distinguished one.

**Focal + Bearing**

One selected alternative remains defined through its contrast.

**Focal + Efficacy**

One focal difference changes what can happen.

**Distributed + Standing**

A criterion holds across a region until the differentiated extent stands.

**Distributed + Bearing**

A distinction remains dependent on its wider context.

**Distributed + Efficacy**

A criterion operates across a field and changes treatment.

**Release + Standing**

Previously conflated possibilities stand independently.

**Release + Bearing**

Flattened variation becomes an ordered relative difference.

**Release + Efficacy**

A criterion excludes what no longer qualifies to proceed.

The semantic forms differ.

But the topology and persistence roles recur predictably.

This is why the family can be read like a conjugation table.

The Transformation Pattern supplies the governing kind of change.

Topology and persistence conjugate it into nine local forms.

### Substitute Opening and the grammar repeats

Now replace Differentiation with Opening.

The topology/persistence pattern remains.

Focal + Standing becomes a source-centred available field.

Focal + Bearing becomes divergence that remains linked to a common source.

Focal + Efficacy becomes branching output.

Distributed + Standing becomes alternate passage.

Distributed + Bearing becomes span-carried reach.

Distributed + Efficacy becomes availability distributed across a field.

Release + Standing becomes a released option.

Release + Bearing becomes continual passage freed from segmentation.

Release + Efficacy becomes effective reach beyond a prior edge.

The meanings are not repeated.

The functional roles are.

That distinction is essential.

Periodicity does not mean the cells are copies.

It means the same grammatical contributions recur under substitution.

### Periodicity appears in generated sentences

Bearing repeatedly leaves the result available through constitutive relation.

Efficacy repeatedly leaves an accomplished capacity or consequence.

The generated syntax therefore carries the periodicity.

The labels reflect it rather than create it.

### The 54 positions form a finite state-space

Here the dimensions are:

Transformation Pattern,

Completion Topology,

Persistence Mode.

The Atlas is not saying an event moves through all 54 positions.

The terms remain phenomenological and functional.

The periodicity belongs to completed-form structure.

It does not describe a temporal cycle through which every event passes.

No position in the state-space is “the true coordinate” in the sense that occupying it makes a claim true.

Truth and success remain separate evaluative dimensions outside the 6 × 3 × 3 grammar.

This makes the grammar increasingly prospective.

The periodic architecture tells us what differences to expect under substitution.

We can now read the Atlas as a periodic state-space.

We know that coordinates generate local forms.

Instead of beginning with an example and finding its coordinate, we can begin with a coordinate and ask:

## From Classification to Prospective Generation

Until now, we have often moved from event to coordinate.

We observe a case.

We identify its opening pressure.

We determine what changed.

We determine what made that change sufficient.

We determine how the result remains available.

Then we locate the coordinate.

That is a valid use of the grammar.

But the architecture permits a second direction.

We can begin with the coordinate and ask what kind of completed event could satisfy it.

The theory calls this **prospective generation**.

### A coordinate is a conjunction of constraints

Take:

**Repair + Distributed + Bearing**

Repair requires a governed misfit to be corrected.

Distributed requires the sufficiency of that correction to depend on a plurality, range, or system.

Bearing requires the achieved result to persist through constitutive relations.

Before consulting any canonical name, we already know a great deal.

The event cannot be merely a focal correction.

It cannot leave only a standing result.

It cannot be primarily about opening new possibility or settling a choice.

Any admissible realization has to satisfy all three constraints simultaneously.

The coordinate therefore defines a structured region of possible forms.

### Generate before looking

A useful test is to hide the canonical crystallization and examples.

Start only with the coordinate.

Write down the contribution of the Transformation Pattern.

Add the topology.

Add the Persistence Mode.

Then attempt one domain-general event-sentence.

Only afterward compare the result with the Atlas.

This procedure matters because it tests whether the coordinate has genuine generative content.

If a cell can only be reconstructed after its familiar name is supplied, then the name is doing too much theoretical work.

If the coordinate itself produces a recognizable event-shape, the grammar is explanatory rather than merely mnemonic.

### Prospective generation is not arbitrary invention

Generation does not mean free creativity.

The coordinate constrains the result.

Take:

**Opening + Focal + Efficacy**

Opening requires increased availability.

Focal requires that completion gather around one privileged source or locus.

Efficacy requires that the achieved result persist as operative consequence or capacity.

A generated sentence might say:

a focal source opens new possibilities in a way that actively produces multiple available continuations.

The Atlas crystallizes this as **Branching output**.

A stem producing shoots, a question producing lines of inquiry, and a railway switching yard producing outgoing paths are manifestations.

The examples are not random.

They satisfy the generated structure.

### Admissible realization

A candidate example is admissible only if it satisfies all three coordinate constraints.

Suppose we propose a case for Opening + Focal + Efficacy in which a road simply stands open.

That may involve Opening.

It may be Focal.

But if the important remainder is merely that the road stands available, the Persistence Mode may be Standing rather than Efficacy.

The grammar therefore does diagnostic work.

It tells us why an attractive example may belong somewhere else.

### Constraint strength affects prospective generation

Near-determinate coordinates generate narrow expectations.

Open realizations generate broader expectations.

Consider **Differentiation + Focal + Standing**.

The space of admissible form is so narrow that indication emerges almost immediately.

Now consider **Differentiation + Focal + Efficacy**.

The coordinate clearly generates operative focal distinction, but more than one semantic realization may fit.

Prospective generation therefore has to respect constraint strength.

It should derive what is structurally forced without pretending to derive what remains semantically free.

### The canonical crystallization becomes a check rather than a premise

This reverses the usual pedagogical direction.

Instead of:

canonical name → explanation → examples,

we can proceed:

coordinate → generated form → compare with canonical crystallization → inspect manifestations.

The canonical crystallization becomes a check on semantic individuation.

Does it fall inside the generated region?

Does it add only what the coordinate permits?

Does it sharpen rather than contradict the generated syntax?

This creates a stronger internal test of the Atlas.

### Manifestations become tests of portability

After generating the form, we can ask whether it can be recognized in more than one domain.

A strong cross-domain manifestation should preserve the event-shape while changing material substantially.

For **Opening + Distributed + Bearing**, bridge, network cable, and suspension bridge are useful because the common structure is maintained reach across a span.

For **Relating + Distributed + Bearing**, dancers, negotiation, and coordinated rowing show mutual determination across different materials.

These comparisons test whether the generated form is genuinely domain-general inside the corpus.

### Prospective generation exposes bad examples

The method can also reject weak manifestations.

An example may resemble a canonical name superficially while failing one coordinate constraint.

A “boundary” example that merely marks a place but has no operative consequence does not satisfy Efficacy.

A “distributed” example with many objects but one focal locus actually doing all the sufficiency work may not be Distributed.

A supposed Release example in which the old governor remains fully active is not completion by Release.

Prospective generation therefore sharpens classification.

### Historical formation still runs in the other direction

Prospective generation should not be confused with predicting the actual sequence of an event.

The coordinate tells us what completed form must be satisfied.

It does not tell us exactly how the event will unfold.

Historical formation may contain detours, provisional structures, local completions, and repairs.

So there are now two clearly different directions:

**coordinate → generated completed form**

and

**historical opening → local operations → governing transformation → landing → persistent result**

The first is structural generation.

The second is historical reconstruction.

### Intelligibility before truth remains a constraint on interpretation

A generated form describes what would count as a completed event of a given structural type.

It still says nothing by itself about whether the content is true.

A false explanation can instantiate a generated form.

A bad plan can instantiate one.

A fictional sequence can instantiate one.

Prospective generation predicts structural admissibility inside the grammar, not truth.

### “Prediction” needs careful language

At this stage it becomes tempting to say the grammar predicts forms.

Internally, that can be reasonable if we mean:

given a coordinate, the grammar predicts what structural roles an admissible form must satisfy.

But that is not yet an empirical prediction in the scientific sense.

An empirical prediction would require held-out cases, independent observation, measurable outcomes, or behavior that could fail to match the theory.

So the safer mature phrase is **prospective generation**.

The grammar generates a structural expectation.

Whether the world, cognition, or independent raters conform to that expectation remains a research question.

### The theory’s strongest internal movement

The framework now supports two-way movement.

From manifestation toward coordinate:

case → structural analysis → coordinate.

From coordinate toward manifestation:

coordinate → generated syntax → admissible region → crystallization → candidate manifestations.

This two-way structure is one of the strongest reasons to call the system a grammar.

A simple taxonomy normally works mainly in the first direction.

A grammar supports constructive movement in the second.

### What remains open

Prospective generation still does not establish universality.

It does not prove that all generated regions correspond to psychologically natural categories.

It does not prove that independent analysts will generate the same sentence from a coordinate.

It does not prove that the canonical crystallization is uniquely optimal.

Those are testable questions.

The internal achievement is narrower:

the coordinate architecture is rich enough to support systematic prospective derivation.

### The next threshold

A theory capable of prospective generation has reached a different explanatory level from the one with which the project began.

The original work emerged from sentence resolution and a 6 × 3 × 3 geometry of Work, Orientation, and Survivor.

The mature grammar now treats those coordinates as generators of completed form.

To understand the full significance of that shift, we have to reconstruct the theory’s own history.

## How the Theory Changed Without Losing Its Skeleton

The mature theory can make its own history look inevitable.

Once the dimensions are called Transformation Pattern, Completion Topology, and Persistence Mode, the architecture seems almost self-explanatory.

But that was not how the project began.

It began with sentences.

More specifically, it began with the problem of how a sentence becomes complete enough to be taken up as a whole.

Understanding the distance between that beginning and the mature grammar helps clarify what the theory now claims to explain.

### The original observational object was the unfolding sentence

A sentence unfolds in time.

Early words can be locally intelligible while the larger structure remains unresolved.

A later phrase can reorganize what came before.

A provisional reading can fail.

A final attribution can wrap an earlier statement.

A condition can change the role of two already meaningful clauses.

These phenomena forced attention toward history.

The initial theoretical drama was:

sentence opens → local operations occur → one organization becomes governing → the sentence lands → a result remains available.

The Atlas was reached from the sentence.

### The first stable geometry

The early 6 × 3 × 3 system used different names.

The first axis was **Work**:

- Distinction
- Expansion
- Relation
- Correction
- Integration
- Rest

The second was **Orientation**:

- Toward
- Across
- Away

The third was **Survivor**:

- Is
- With
- Does

These terms were powerful because they captured recurring phenomenological roles.

They were also incomplete.

Their exact theoretical status had not yet been fully understood.

### Work became Transformation Pattern

The early Work axis asked what the sentence or situation was doing in order to complete.

Distinction became **Differentiation**.

Expansion became **Opening**.

Relation became **Relating**.

Correction became **Repair**.

Integration remained **Integration**.

Rest became **Settlement**.

The shift was not merely cosmetic.

Transformation Pattern states more exactly that the axis identifies the decisive kind of change between unresolved and completed organization.

The mature names describe functional invariants rather than intuitive “work” labels.

### Orientation became Completion Topology

Toward, Across, and Away captured real structure.

Toward often involved a centre, source, pivot, target, or endpoint.

Across involved span, plurality, sequence, or distributed organization.

Away involved escape, removal, reversal, release, or loss of a prior constraint.

But “Orientation” invited a directional reading.

It sounded like where an event moved.

The mature theory recognized that the deeper role was different.

The axis specifies **what makes the transformation sufficient as a completion**.

Toward became **Focal**.

Across became **Distributed**.

Away became **Release**.

Release is especially revealing.

The mature concept is not simply movement away from something.

It is completion through loss of prior governance.

The change in vocabulary therefore marks a change in theoretical level.

### Survivor became Persistence Mode

Is, With, and Does were compact.

They captured three different remainders.

Something stands.

Something remains through relation.

Something remains as operation or effect.

But “Survivor” can suggest a thing left over after an event.

The mature account is broader.

**Standing**, **Bearing**, and **Efficacy** describe modes of achieved availability.

The result may be an object, relation, rule, capacity, effect, or changed possibility.

Persistence Mode therefore expresses the functional role more precisely than Survivor.

### The 6 × 3 × 3 skeleton survived

This is what makes the intellectual history unusual.

The mature theory did not discard the original architecture.

The 6 × 3 × 3 geometry survived.

What changed was the interpretation of what the axes were doing.

The early system treated coordinates largely as a way to resolve completed sentences into a structured Atlas.

The mature theory treats coordinates as compositional constraints that generate completed forms.

The skeleton remained.

Its explanatory meaning deepened.

### The early Atlas made the cells look primary

Once 54 vivid canonical forms had been named, they became highly salient.

Focal mark.

Binary bearing.

Boundary act.

Scope standing.

Span-carried reach.

Reciprocal shift.

And many others.

This encouraged a classificatory interpretation:

there are 54 recurrent forms, and the coordinates tell us where they belong.

That was already substantial.

But it left a deeper question unanswered.

Do the coordinates merely classify the forms?

Or do the coordinates explain why those forms have the structure they do?

The mature theory gives generative priority to the coordinate-generated form.

### Generated form became the deeper local object

Once the coordinate-generated event-sentence could be written independently of the canonical name, the theoretical priority changed.

The coordinate does not merely point to a cell.

It constrains a phenomenological syntax.

That generated form is more fundamental than the canonical crystallization.

The canonical form remains useful, but it becomes representational rather than primitive.

This shift explains why the mature theory calls itself a **constraint grammar**.

### Sentence resolution changed status too

Sentences remain important.

They are historically privileged because their unfolding makes opening pressure, local completion, reanalysis, governing transformation, landing, and persistence visible.

But the mature theory no longer treats sentence processing as the ontology of the system.

A bridge, machine, decision, organization, mathematical event, or other situation can exhibit completed intelligibility without being a sentence.

Sentence resolution became an observational window rather than the object itself.

### The theory moved from recovery to generation

The early dominant direction was:

sentence history → coordinate.

The mature theory adds:

coordinate → generated form.

This reversibility changes the status of the architecture.

Coordinates are no longer merely descriptors recovered from examples.

They become generative constraints.

The Atlas becomes one representation of a grammar.

### Historical vocabulary remains useful only when marked

The old terms should not be erased.

They belong to the theory’s intellectual history.

They can illuminate how the mature distinctions emerged.

But new theoretical writing should not use Work, Orientation, and Survivor as though they were exact synonyms for the mature axes.

The shift in vocabulary records a real conceptual refinement.

Historical reconstruction should preserve that difference.

### The same lossless principle applies to the theory’s own development

The developmental essays use lossless simplification.

Earlier descriptions should remain true enough to be sharpened later.

The theory’s own history has a similar shape.

The early system was not simply false.

It captured real recurrent distinctions.

The mature theory clarified their functional roles, changed their names, and altered the explanatory priority among coordinate, generated form, crystallization, and manifestation.

The earlier architecture was reinterpreted rather than destroyed.

### Academic implications

This history also matters for disciplinary placement.

A theory discovered through sentences may look linguistic.

A theory represented by a grid may look taxonomic.

A theory using state-space language may look mathematical.

A theory describing recognizable event-shapes may look phenomenological.

But the mature object has to be located by what it explains, not by the route through which it was discovered.

That object is completed intelligibility.

The academic question therefore becomes:

what field is prepared to study recurrent structural forms by which a structured incompletion becomes sufficiently complete for determinate uptake?

That is the next threshold.

### Claims boundary

The intellectual history demonstrates a change in theoretical self-understanding.

It does not provide independent empirical validation.

The fact that the mature system explains the early system elegantly is evidence of internal coherence.

It is not evidence that the architecture is psychologically universal.

Historical reconstruction and empirical testing remain different tasks.

### The next question

We now know what the theory has become.

It is not primarily a sentence taxonomy.

It is not primarily a list of 54 names.

It is a constraint-compositional architecture of completed intelligibility represented as a finite periodic state-space.

The next task is to ask where such a theory belongs academically, what neighboring fields it resembles, and where those resemblances stop.

## What Kind of Theory Is This?

A theory should be located by the object it studies, not by the vocabulary it happens to use.

The word “grammar” does not automatically make a theory linguistics.

The word “phenomenological” does not automatically make it classical phenomenology.

The use of a state-space does not automatically make the theory mathematics.

The use of constraints does not automatically make it systems theory.

The possibility of computational implementation does not automatically make it artificial intelligence.

The mature Constraint Grammar therefore needs an explicit academic location.

### The object of study

The theory studies:

**recurrent structural forms by which a structured incompletion becomes sufficiently complete for determinate uptake.**

That formulation contains the whole architecture in compressed form.

There is an unresolved field with opening pressure.

A decisive Transformation Pattern changes the relevant incompletion.

A Completion Topology makes that change sufficient.

A Persistence Mode specifies how the achieved result remains available.

The coordinate generates a phenomenological event-syntax.

A canonical crystallization sharpens that region semantically.

Concrete manifestations realize it in particular materials.

Historical formation describes how the result emerged.

Truth, correctness, and success remain later evaluative questions.

### Not primarily linguistics

The theory was discovered through sentences.

Sentences remain a privileged observational domain.

But the mature invariants are not phrase structures, dependency relations, grammatical constructions, or lexical classes.

The same coordinate-generated form can appear in physical, practical, organizational, narrative, mathematical, or linguistic material.

So linguistics is an important historical source and testing domain.

It is not the ontology of the theory.

### Not primarily semantics

The theory does not classify subject matter or semantic content.

Different contents can share one completed form.

Similar contents can occupy different coordinates depending on what changes, what makes the change sufficient, and what remains.

The distinction between coordinate-generated form and canonical crystallization makes this especially clear.

The grammar is structural before it is semantically individuated.

### Not primarily epistemology

The theory places intelligibility before truth and justification.

A false diagnosis can be structurally complete.

A mistaken explanation can be fully intelligible.

A fictional event can be well formed.

Epistemology asks whether understanding, belief, explanation, or knowledge is warranted.

The Constraint Grammar asks an earlier structural question:

how did a situation become determinate enough to support such evaluation?

The two domains are related but not identical.

### Closest descriptive neighbor: phenomenology

The generated forms describe recognizable ways a situation can appear and change as unresolved, completed, held, operative, related, or released.

That gives the framework a genuine phenomenological character.

But the theory is not simply classical phenomenology.

It introduces a finite Cartesian architecture.

It treats coordinates compositionally.

It uses generated sentences.

It distinguishes constraint strength.

It proposes cross-domain empirical tests.

Phenomenology is therefore a close descriptive neighbor rather than a complete disciplinary identity.

### Strongest current parent: theoretical cognitive science

Cognitive science is broad enough to host a theory that may ultimately connect philosophical analysis, psychological testing, linguistic evidence, computational modeling, and artificial-intelligence implementation.

The Constraint Grammar does not presently specify a cognitive mechanism.

But it proposes a candidate level of organization that cognitive science could investigate.

This makes theoretical cognitive science a plausible parent discipline.

### Philosophy of cognitive science as immediate intellectual home

At present, the framework is strongest as a theory of explanatory level and structural organization.

It proposes a state-space and functional distinctions.

It does not yet specify neural circuits, memory stores, learning rules, algorithms, or processing mechanisms.

That makes philosophy of cognitive science an especially natural immediate home.

The theory asks what kind of organization should be posited before lower-level mechanism is specified.

### Functional-phenomenological level

The most accurate compact description is:

**a functional-phenomenological theory of completion structure.**

“Functional” means that the axes are individuated by the roles they play in moving a situation from unresolvedness to sufficient completion.

“Phenomenological” means that the generated forms describe recognizable organizations of appearance and uptake.

“Structural” means that the invariants are intended to survive changes of domain content.

This is a level description, not a claim that the theory belongs exclusively to one philosophical tradition.

### Relation to cognitive psychology

Cognitive psychology could test whether people actually discriminate the dimensions.

Do independent raters classify cases similarly?

Do people respond differently to Focal, Distributed, and Release completions?

Do Standing, Bearing, and Efficacy correspond to behavioral distinctions?

Does training improve reliability?

These are empirical questions.

Cognitive psychology is therefore a major testing domain.

The present theory itself is not yet a psychological mechanism.

### Relation to computation and AI

The grammar is naturally implementable.

A system could attempt to classify opening pressure, transformation, topology, persistence, generated form, and constraint strength.

Models could generate candidate manifestations from coordinates.

Human and model judgments could be compared.

But computational implementability does not make the theory an AI theory.

A program can instantiate a framework without providing its academic identity.

AI is an application and testing domain unless future work establishes a deeper computational account.

### Relation to systems theory

Some manifestations involve systems.

Distributed completion and Integration can look especially systemic.

But other coordinates are focal and local.

No general systems ontology is required by the formal statement of the grammar.

Systems theory may offer comparisons or implementation tools.

It does not presently contain the object.

### Relation to mathematics

The 6 × 3 × 3 architecture is formal.

The state-space is finite.

The periodicity is systematic.

The grammar supports substitution.

But the framework is not yet mathematical in the strong sense of an axiomatized calculus.

Its primitives remain functional-phenomenological.

Constraint strength is qualitative.

Admissible regions are conceptually rather than metrically defined.

Mathematics provides representation and possible future formalization.

It is not yet the theory’s disciplinary home.

### A Marr-like comparison

The framework can be compared loosely with task-level or functional descriptions in cognitive science.

It specifies what kind of unresolved organization changes into what kind of completed availability.

It does not specify the algorithm that performs the transformation or the physical substrate implementing it.

This makes the theory higher-level than mechanism.

But the comparison should remain limited.

The grammar does not yet provide mathematically specified input-output mappings in the strong computational sense.

So “Marr-like” is a location aid, not an identity.

A useful academic map distinguishes several relations.

# Draft: The Eighteen-Year-Old Edition

## What the Theory Has Shown—and What It Still Has to Earn

At the mature level, the Constraint Grammar of Completed Intelligibility can be stated compactly.

It models recurrent structural forms by which an initially unresolved situation becomes sufficiently complete for determinate uptake.

Its primitive compositional rule is:

**Transformation Pattern + Completion Topology + Persistence Mode → coordinate-generated phenomenological syntax.**

There are six Transformation Patterns:

Differentiation, Opening, Relating, Repair, Integration, Settlement.

There are three Completion Topologies:

Focal, Distributed, Release.

There are three Persistence Modes:

Standing, Bearing, Efficacy.

Their Cartesian product yields a 6 × 3 × 3 state-space of fifty-four coordinate positions.

But a mature statement of the theory must do more than recite the architecture.

It must distinguish what the corpus demonstrates internally from what remains an external research claim.

### Completed intelligibility

The target phenomenon is **completed intelligibility**.

A situation is complete in the relevant sense when it has become sufficiently determinate, relative to its governing opening pressure, to support stable next uptake without owing the same unresolved work that organized its opening.

Completion is therefore indexed to a demand.

It is not absolute finality.

A completed route can be travelled farther.

A repaired device can later fail.

A settled decision can be reopened.

A sentence can enter a larger discourse.

A theory can resolve one internal question while leaving empirical validation open.

Completion is structural sufficiency, not the end of history.

### Opening pressure

An unresolved situation is not mere absence.

It is already structured enough to place a determinate demand on further uptake.

The theory calls this **opening pressure**.

Opening pressure is the negative contour of the completion to come.

A missing operative difference points toward Differentiation.

Restricted availability points toward Opening.

Absent constitutive bearing points toward Relating.

Governed misfit points toward Repair.

Unorganized plurality points toward Integration.

Residual demand points toward Settlement.

This does not fully determine the later coordinate.

It constrains the relevant Transformation Pattern.

### Transformation Pattern

Transformation Pattern specifies what kind of unresolvedness changes.

**Differentiation:** a difference that was unavailable, flattened, conflated, or non-operative becomes determinate enough to matter.

**Opening:** availability, reach, continuation, or possibility becomes greater than under the opening constraint.

**Relating:** a bearing, dependency, correspondence, ordering, reciprocity, or mutual determination becomes constitutive.

**Repair:** an organization failing relative to an operative criterion is redirected, reweighted, replaced, reversed, or released from the fault.

**Integration:** parts acquire determinate roles through organization into a whole.

**Settlement:** a live demand for further uptake, choice, explanation, adjudication, completion, or continuation is discharged.

These are not sequential stages.

They are alternative governing kinds of transformation.

### Completion Topology

Completion Topology specifies what makes the transformation sufficient.

**Focal:** completion gathers around a privileged locus.

**Distributed:** completion requires a constitutive plurality, span, sequence, range, reciprocal field, or system.

**Release:** completion occurs because something that previously governed the field no longer governs it.

Release is therefore a topology of de-governance.

It is not ordinary motion away from a centre.

The positive completed organization becomes possible because an old constraint loses its determining force.

### Persistence Mode

Persistence Mode specifies how the achieved completion remains available.

**Standing:** the achieved determination itself remains available as something that now stands.

**Bearing:** the result remains through a constitutive relation, dependency, contrast, condition, frame, source, proportion, or mutual support.

**Efficacy:** the result remains as accomplished capacity, operative effect, changed continuation, rule, function, or consequential difference.

Persistence Mode is not tense or duration.

It is the mode of post-completion availability.

### Functional independence inside the grammar

The three dimensions answer different questions.

Transformation Pattern:

**what kind of unresolvedness changed?**

Completion Topology:

**what structural condition made that change sufficient?**

Persistence Mode:

**in what mode does the achieved result remain available?**

Their distinction is supported internally by recurrence under substitution.

Hold one axis fixed and vary the others.

A recognizable invariant remains.

This is the operational sense in which the contributions are independent within the grammar.

That claim should not be silently upgraded.

Empirical independence remains open.

The dimensions could in principle collapse under external observation.

### Coordinate-generated phenomenological syntax

The deepest local theoretical object is not the canonical cell name.

It is the **coordinate-generated form**.

A coordinate constrains a domain-general phenomenological event-syntax before canonical naming.

The three axes jointly specify:

- the governing transformation;
- the topology of sufficiency;
- the mode of achieved availability.

That conjunction defines an admissible region of completed form.

A coordinate-generated sentence makes the region explicit.

### Structural determination and semantic individuation

The coordinate constrains what can count.

That is **structural determination**.

But structural determination does not always uniquely determine a canonical semantic realization.

The further sharpening by which one recognizable phenomenon is selected inside the generated region is **semantic individuation**.

This distinction explains why canonical crystallizations can be strongly guided without being logically forced in every detail.

### Constraint strength and semantic freedom

The Atlas therefore uses three broad constraint-strength judgments:

**Near-determinate**

The coordinate leaves little beyond wording, domain, or minor semantic specification.

**Tightly constrained**

The coordinate defines a narrow family while the crystallization contributes meaningful semantic sharpening.

**Open realization**

The coordinate fixes a recognizable syntax while leaving broader semantic latitude.

That remaining latitude is **semantic freedom**.

It is bounded.

It is not arbitrariness.

### Canonical crystallization

A **canonical crystallization** is a selected, stable, semantically specific realization of a coordinate-generated form.

It makes the region memorable and operationally useful.

It is representationally important.

But it is not theoretically prior to the generated form.

The direction of priority is:

**coordinate → coordinate-generated form → canonical crystallization → concrete manifestation**

### Concrete manifestation

A **concrete manifestation** is a particular case in specific material.

A bridge can manifest span-carried reach.

A network cable can manifest the same form.

A keyring can manifest focal distinguished standing.

A sentence can manifest a completed form.

Manifestations demonstrate recognizability and recurrence within the corpus.

They do not define the coordinate.

### Periodic state-space

The 54 positions form a nested periodic architecture.

Transformation Pattern supplies the slow nine-position rhythm.

Completion Topology supplies the three-position rhythm.

Persistence Mode supplies the one-position Standing/Bearing/Efficacy cycle.

The periodicity occurs in functional roles and generated sentence structure, not merely in table labels.

This supports the description of the framework as a finite periodic state-space.

But “finite” and “periodic” do not imply metaphysical exhaustiveness.

Nor does the current formalism constitute a complete axiomatized calculus.

### Prospective generation

The grammar supports prospective use.

Given a coordinate, one can derive a domain-general structural expectation before consulting its canonical crystallization or manifestations.

This is stronger than mnemonic classification.

It allows:

coordinate → generated syntax → admissible region → candidate crystallization → manifestations.

But prospective generation is an internal theoretical operation.

It should not be confused with empirical prediction unless tested against independent data.

### Historical formation

Completed form must be distinguished from the history that produced it.

The historical layer includes:

- opening;
- local operations and local completions;
- governing transformation;
- landing;
- persistent result.

The coordinate describes achieved organization.

Formation history describes the path by which that organization became governing.

The same coordinate can be reached through different histories.

Similar histories can yield different coordinates.

Sentences are a privileged observational case because their unfolding makes these differences visible.

But sentence processing is not the ontology of the grammar.

### Intelligibility before truth

The framework places intelligibility before truth, correctness, justification, or success in explanatory order.

A false claim can be intelligible.

A fictional event can be complete.

A poor plan can be determinate.

A mistaken diagnosis can be structurally settled.

The theory asks how a result becomes sufficiently determinate to be judged.

It does not replace the later judgment.

Completed intelligibility is therefore not epistemic endorsement.

### Academic location

The strongest current placement is:

**a functional-phenomenological theory of completion structure situated primarily within theoretical cognitive science and the philosophy of cognitive science, with phenomenology as its closest descriptive neighbor.**

The framework is not primarily:

- linguistics;
- semantics;
- epistemology;
- systems theory;
- artificial intelligence;
- mathematics;
- or a theory of neural mechanism.

Those fields may provide neighbors, testing domains, formal tools, or implementations.

The theory’s direct object remains completion structure.

### What the corpus currently demonstrates

Within its own publication, the project demonstrates:

- a coherent 6 × 3 × 3 compositional architecture;
- a complete 54-position structural Atlas;
- a deterministic one-to-one correspondence between Atlas records and the 54 explanatory companion texts;
- mature terminology distinguishing generated form, crystallization, and manifestation;
- repeated use of the same coordinate vocabulary across the corpus;
- coordinate-generated sentences exhibiting systematic compositional roles;
- nested periodicity across the state-space;
- a coherent distinction between completed form and historical formation.

These are substantial internal achievements.

They establish organization and theoretical coherence within the corpus.

They do not by themselves establish psychological, causal, empirical, or metaphysical reality.

### What remains hypothesis

The following remain research claims:

- that the three dimensions correspond to psychologically real distinctions in human cognition;
- that the coordinate structure recurs reliably across domains not used to develop the framework;
- that trained independent raters can classify held-out cases with useful agreement;
- that the framework generalizes beyond the originating examples;
- that the 54-position space is exhaustive rather than productive but incomplete;
- that Transformation Pattern, Completion Topology, and Persistence Mode remain empirically independent under observation;
- that the grammar yields predictive or diagnostic value beyond simpler descriptions.

These claims require external evidence.

### What has not been proven

The publication does not presently establish:

- metaphysical necessity;
- empirical universality;
- unique classification for every possible case;
- a causal mechanism producing the proposed structure;
- clinical effectiveness;
- commercial effectiveness.

A mature presentation should state these absences without embarrassment.

The absence of proof is not a flaw if the claim is not being made.

### What would count as evidence

Useful evidence should expose the grammar to uncertainty and alternatives.

Examples include:

- blind classification studies using cases withheld from theory development;
- inter-rater agreement before and after explicit training;
- held-out examples from domains absent from the originating corpus;
- direct comparison with simpler or competing taxonomies;
- ablation studies removing one or more dimensions;
- tests of predictive or diagnostic value against baseline descriptions;
- comparisons between human and model classifications;
- detailed analysis of disagreement rather than averaging it away;
- independent cross-domain replication.

The theory becomes stronger when it survives opportunities to fail.

### What would weaken or falsify the framework

The grammar should be weakened, revised, or rejected if evidence repeatedly shows that its structure does not earn its complexity.

That could include:

- systematic cases that cannot be represented without ad hoc repair;
- persistently low inter-rater reliability even after clear training;
- empirical collapse of supposedly independent dimensions;
- a simpler alternative taxonomy explaining the same observations as well or better;
- failure to predict, discriminate, or diagnose completion structure better than reasonable baselines.

These are not threats to be hidden.

They are part of the research programme.

### Further research-status precision

## Internal coherence is not trivial, but it is not external truth

There is a temptation to understate internal achievement once empirical validation is demanded.

That would also be a mistake.

A coherent architecture with explicit primitives, exhaustive internal combinations, generated forms, canonical correspondences, constraint-strength distinctions, and a historical layer is a real theoretical construction.

It can be inspected for contradiction.

It can be compared with alternatives.

It can generate hypotheses.

It can support reproducible editorial analysis.

Internal coherence is therefore necessary intellectual work.

What it cannot do is validate itself externally.

The distinction is not between “real science” and “mere theory.”

It is between different stages and kinds of warrant.

## Exhaustiveness is one of the most important open questions

The Cartesian product is exhaustive **given the chosen primitive values**.

Once six Transformation Patterns, three Completion Topologies, and three Persistence Modes are fixed, all combinations are represented.

But that is not the same as proving that the primitive sets themselves exhaust the phenomenon.

A seventh Transformation Pattern may someday be required.

A fourth topology might prove necessary.

A proposed Persistence Mode might split.

Or empirical work might show that two present values collapse.

The Atlas is formally complete relative to its stated primitives.

Phenomenological or empirical exhaustiveness remains open.

That distinction should be explicit whenever “complete 54-cell Atlas” is mentioned.

## Unique recoverability is also open

Even if the state-space is adequate, difficult cases may support more than one plausible coordinate.

Some events may be genuinely nested.

Some descriptions may foreground different governing pressures.

Some formation histories may permit multiple legitimate levels of analysis.

A mature classification programme therefore has to distinguish:
- ambiguity caused by poor definitions;
- disagreement caused by inadequate training;
- genuinely multi-level or nested structure;
- cases that expose weaknesses in the grammar.

Unique recoverability cannot be assumed in advance.

It has to be investigated.

## Ablation is a particularly important test

Because the grammar claims three functionally distinct dimensions, one strong empirical strategy is to remove one.

Can a simpler 6 × 3 system explain the same cases without meaningful loss?

Can Transformation Pattern and Completion Topology be collapsed?

Can Bearing be absorbed into Standing or Efficacy?

If a reduced model performs as well as the full grammar, the extra complexity is not earning its place.

This is the empirical analogue of the substitution reasoning introduced much earlier in the developmental sequence.

A useful component should make a difference when removed.

## Inter-rater reliability is necessary but not sufficient

If trained analysts cannot classify cases with useful agreement, the theory has a serious problem.

But high agreement alone would not prove the grammar is right.

People can learn to apply a coherent but unhelpful coding scheme consistently.

Reliability must therefore be combined with validity.

The classifications should discriminate meaningful structures, generalize to held-out cases, and ideally support prediction or diagnosis beyond simpler baselines.

This is why the research programme needs several kinds of evidence rather than one decisive metric.

## Competing taxonomies are essential

The theory should not be tested only against itself.

A simpler framework may capture most of the same regularities.

An established taxonomy from a neighboring field may explain the observations more efficiently.

A data-driven clustering method may reveal a different structure.

Comparison with alternatives is essential because complexity has to earn explanatory value.

The question is not merely whether the Constraint Grammar can describe cases.

Many sufficiently flexible systems can.

The question is whether its three-dimensional architecture explains or predicts structure better than reasonable rivals.

## Human-model comparison can reveal the shape of disagreement

Artificial-intelligence systems offer an unusual testing opportunity.

Humans and models can be given the same definitions and held-out cases.

Agreement may support the clarity of the framework.

Disagreement may be more informative.

Does the model rely too heavily on lexical cues?

Do humans privilege formation history over completed form?

Do both groups confuse certain topologies?

Do disagreements cluster in Open realization cells?

The aim should not be to average differences away.

The pattern of disagreement can expose where the grammar is underspecified.

## Falsification should be local as well as global

The theory need not fail all at once.

A particular Transformation Pattern may prove unstable.

A subset of cells may collapse.

One canonical crystallization may be poorly chosen.

Constraint-strength judgments may need revision.

The historical layer may prove useful while the 54-cell exhaustiveness claim weakens.

A mature research programme should allow partial revision.

That is healthier than forcing every challenge into an all-or-nothing verdict.

## The strongest thesis available now

After all qualifications, the present strongest defensible thesis remains substantial:

the corpus specifies a coherent finite compositional architecture in which three recurring functional constraints generate a structured state-space of completed intelligibility, with explicit distinctions among generated form, semantic crystallization, concrete manifestation, and historical formation.

That is what has been earned internally.

Whether this architecture is psychologically real, empirically general, uniquely recoverable, causally implemented, or exhaustive remains to be earned externally.

The theory is strongest when both sentences are said together.

### The completed ascent

We can now look back at the beginning.

A red ball under one of three cups.

A locked door.

A broken wheel.

A pile of parts.

A choice that has not yet been made.

The earliest description said that an unfinished situation asks for something, and that the right kind of event can make the asking stop.

Nothing in the mature theory requires us to retract that.

We can now see exactly what it compressed.

The asking was opening pressure.

The kind of answer was Transformation Pattern.

What made it enough was Completion Topology.

What remained was Persistence Mode.

The combination generated a local phenomenological syntax.

The canonical name crystallized that syntax.

The example manifested it.

The history explained how it formed.

Truth and justification remained later questions.

The academic position located the level of analysis.

The Research Status marked the boundary between demonstrated architecture and open hypothesis.

The simple account was not a metaphor that had to be discarded.

It was the mature theory seen from very far away.

And, of course, we knew all along that you were never five.
